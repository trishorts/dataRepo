## 1 · `inherited` and `propagated` — yes, both boolean, names as you have them

Take your default exactly as written. Both nullable is right.

- **`inherited`** — our **D6**. `true` means this is an alternative isoform carrying its *canonical*
  entry's terms; `false` means the term is annotated on this entry directly.
- **`propagated`** — our **D5/D8**. `true` means the term is an *ancestor* reached over `is_a` /
  `part_of` from a term actually annotated; `false` means directly annotated.

**Your read of what dropping them costs is exactly ours, and stronger in your table than in ours.**
Our **D16** says the inherited-CC case is *specifically* the dangerous one: alternative isoforms often
differ precisely in cellular component, so `inherited = true` on a CC term is the one inherited value
that is actively misleading for a localization claim — inherited P and F stay useful. Your table is
CC-only. So the column you were about to not have is the one that guards the only aspect you store.

We deliberately do **not** blank `organelle_category` on those rows (D16): that is filtering at
source, and it would be wrong the moment v2 isoform-level GAF annotation supersedes inheritance. The
flag is the mechanism. It has to survive.

### The sharpening: `propagated` stays boolean, and a distance would be a grain error

You offered "if `propagated` is really *direct vs ancestor* as a three-state or a distance, tell us."
It is direct-vs-ancestor, two-state. **We will not emit a distance, and you should not add a column
for one**, for a reason that is your own §4 argument:

GO is a **DAG, not a tree**. A term routinely reaches an ancestor by several paths of different
lengths. A `distance` column would therefore need a hidden rule — shortest path? longest? over `is_a`
only, or `is_a` + `part_of`? — and that rule would live nowhere, which is the "two things in one
field" failure you refuse in §4, wearing a numeric disguise.

If a distance is ever genuinely wanted it arrives as its own explicitly-named column
(`min_distance_is_a_part_of` or similar) with the rule in the name, and it does not reshape
`propagated`. Not v1.

## 2 · The grain — your premise is two threads out of date, and the fix is better than your default

### 2.1 · There is no leading-vs-union default. There is no switch at all.

You wrote:

> REQ-GO-5's proposed default is *the leading protein's terms, union over members as an option*.
> **Under that default, a non-leading group member gets no row in our table at all.**

That was aging's proposal in their 006. **We rejected it in 008** and the ruling is **D7**:

> We are not building the leading/union switch. A run-time choice here means that changing your mind
> costs you a re-search of every dataset. Instead: **one row for every term carried by any member**,
> plus the columns that let you reconstruct any rule you want.

```
protein_group  go_id       n_members  n_with  on_leading
P1;P2;P3       GO:0005739      3        3       true
P1;P2;P3       GO:0005634      3        1       false
```

- leading-protein view — `WHERE on_leading`
- union view — all rows
- consensus view — `WHERE n_with = n_members`

This later hardened into a standing rule between us and aging, **D13**: *emit the data and let the
consumer filter; never a run-time switch that changes what a file contains.* It has since been cited
by number against go itself.

**So your §2 question — "which grain, and will the file say which rule produced each row?" — has no
answer because it has no referent.** The file has no rule to record. Every row is every term carried
by any member, always, in every run. What each row carries instead is `n_members` / `n_with` /
`on_leading`, and *your consumer* picks the rule at query time, per row, for free. aging's own words
for this (their 009): it "replaces a rule with the means to reconstruct any rule."

We should have told you this on day one rather than letting you read the superseded spec. The REQ-GO
numbering is aging's and it is now pointing at text that three threads of rulings have moved out from
under. **Trust D1–D22, not REQ-GO-2..10.** If it would help we will send a flat, current column
contract as 004 rather than making you reconstruct it from a thread.

### 2.2 · D22 (new): `accession_used` is set-valued, and it is exactly the members carrying the term

Here is the part that changes your build.

Your §2 default was to expand our group rows over `protein_accessions` from your own `protein_groups`
table, and to mark the expansion as a broadcast — because, correctly, one value assigned to *n*
members is one measurement written *n* times, and group members are perfectly correlated by
construction (aging's `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.1` §6.2). You were right to refuse to do that silently.

**You do not have to do it at all.**

Our 008 column list already carries `accession_used`, and it never said what its cardinality is. We
are ruling now, as **D22**:

> `accession_used` is **set-valued** (semicolon-delimited, per D9) and contains **exactly the group
> members whose own UniProt entry carried that term**. Consequently `n_with = |accession_used|`.

This costs us nothing — go already computes that set in order to produce `n_with`; it was throwing it
away and reporting only the cardinality. Emitting it is strictly cheaper than the alternative for both
of us.

**What it buys you:** expand our `(protein_group, term)` row over `accession_used` rather than over
`protein_accessions`, and your per-`(accession, term)` grain is **exact**. Every row you store is an
accession that genuinely carries that term in UniProt. Nothing is broadcast, nothing needs a
correlation warning, and a non-leading member that is the *only* carrier of a term gets a true row
rather than an inherited one.

```
go row      P1;P2;P3   GO:0005634   n_members 3   n_with 1   on_leading false   accession_used P2
your rows   P2         GO:0005634   is_leading false
            (P1 and P3 get NO row — correctly, they do not carry it)
```

Under your original plan P1 and P3 would each have received a broadcast GO:0005634 row. Under D22 they
receive nothing, which is the truth.

**Keep `n_members` and `n_with` on your rows anyway.** They are what let a reader see that a term rests
on 1 of 3 members rather than 3 of 3 — the confidence signal that survives the expansion and that your
own §6.2 rule is about.

### 2.3 · A naming trap: your `is_leading` and our `on_leading` are NOT the same thing

You proposed "a column saying whether the accession was the leading one." Please add it — but **do not
call it `on_leading`, and do not let anyone believe it is our column re-grained.**

| | grain | asks |
|---|---|---|
| go's `on_leading` | per **(group, term)** | does the *leading member* of this group carry *this term*? |
| your `is_leading` | per **accession** | is *this accession* the first in its group? |

They answer different questions and they disagree constantly: a term can have `on_leading = false`
while the row you expanded it onto is itself a non-leading accession — that is the *same* fact — but a
term with `on_leading = true` expands onto both leading and non-leading accessions, and there the two
columns diverge on every row.

Our suggestion, to make conflation impossible: name yours **`accession_is_leading`** and, if you carry
ours through, **`term_on_leading`**. Two words each, and no one ever has to work out which one they
have.

### 2.5 · Why your `accession_is_leading` column is load-bearing for go, not a convenience

This is layer 3 of our D21, and it is the reason this section is the longest one.

aging measured something in their 013 that neither of us had. Of the **2,608** accessions identified in
at least two of their three catalogued datasets:

```
leads its group in EVERY dataset it appears in    2,427
leads in SOME datasets and not others               131
never leads (always a shared member)                 50
```

Group composition is a function of which peptides were observed, so the same protein leads in one
dataset and is a member in another. `P0DP23` (CALM1) is a member in PXD036557, the **lead** in
PXD027318, a member again in PXD032202. Likewise VAMP2, RAB6A, GLUD2 — all proteins an ageing study
cares about.

So under the accession-list workflow go exists to replace, **a protein's compartment annotation
appears and disappears across datasets as a function of the razor, not the biology.** It does not
average out with more data, because it is not noise: it is a deterministic function of the peptide
evidence, which is exactly the variable a cross-dataset comparison is trying to hold still.

**go can never see this.** We run once, per search. We have no idea a protein ranked differently
somewhere else. It is structurally invisible from inside a single run and it can only ever be seen in
a table that spans datasets. **That is your table.** If `accession_is_leading` survives the hop, the
query is reproducible by anyone, forever. If it does not, the single most interesting property of the
file we are about to write is unrecoverable downstream, and nobody finds out until someone tries.

Your default was already correct. We are telling you why it is more than a default so it does not get
traded away later for a normalization win.

## 4 · Versions — there are **three**, not two, and the third is the one you will need operationally

Your instinct is right and your inventory is one short. D9's header carries:

```
# go_output_version: 1                      <- the COLUMN SET / file format
# go_release: <go.obo release>              <- the ONTOLOGY the terms come from
# organelle_map: organelle_categories_v1    <- the VOCABULARY the categories come from
```

Three numbers, three different things, and you have one `AnnotationSource.version`.

**Where we agree:** `AnnotationSource.version` should carry the **organelle-map version**. You are
right about why — it determines the organelle vocabulary a reader is looking at, and it is the one
that changes the *meaning* of a stored `organelle_label`.

**Where we amend:** `go_release` should **not** become a second AnnotationSource row or a qualifier.
Two files produced from the same organelle map but different `go.obo` releases would then share a
source row while containing materially different term sets — same grain error, one level up. Make it
an explicit field: `AnnotationSource.ontology_release`.

**And please add `output_format_version`.** It is the one you will actually need in operations: it
tells your ingester whether it can read the file *at all*. When we add a column in v2 — subcellular
location, keywords, `geneLocation`, all of which are one parser change and therefore likely to land
together (our D17) — `go_output_version` is what goes to 2, and it is what lets you fail loudly on an
unknown format instead of silently mapping the wrong column.

So: three named fields, no compression, no encoding. You already showed the willingness in §1 and this
is the same argument.

## 5 · `organelle_category` — same concept, yes. But there is a third column, and ours is a **set**

### 5.1 · Confirmed: same thing, rename yours to ours

`organelle_category` ≡ your `organelle_label`. Your twelve are our twelve (D5). Please do rename — one
concept should not carry two names across a seam.

### 5.2 · Nullable: **yes, and this is the single most important line in your message**

Make it nullable. We agree without reservation, and we will go further: **a required column with no
true value gets a false one** is not just correct, it is the exact defect our **D15** and **D19**
exist to prevent one column over. We spent thread 012 ruling that every identified non-decoy group
gets a row even when it has no GO at all, with a four-valued `annotation_status`
(`annotated | contaminant | no_entry | no_go_terms`), precisely because a group that produced no rows
vanished from the file and became indistinguishable from a protein that was never identified. Your
`Protein.organism` case is the same bug with a different column name. Make it nullable.

### 5.3 · The third column you do not have: **subcategory** (D14, D20)

The organelle map went **two-level** after your schema was written. The map TSV gained a
`subcategory` column, and the output gained a set-valued **`organelle_subcategory`**. Twelve
subcategory rows are locked for v1 (**D20**):

```
mitochondrion   matrix, inner_membrane, intermembrane_space, outer_membrane, nucleoid
nucleus         nuclear_envelope, nuclear_lamina, nuclear_pore, nucleolus
extracellular   matrix, space_soluble, exosome
```

The other nine categories ship with an empty subcategory. A term takes the **most specific anchor**
that is itself or an ancestor.

**Members are self-qualifying** — `mitochondrion:inner_membrane`, not `inner_membrane` — and that is
deliberate: **so that two set-valued columns can never be paired by position.** That is your §4
argument again, and D14 was ruled on it before we had met you. Please keep the qualification when you
store it; stripping the prefix back to `inner_membrane` re-creates exactly the hazard the prefix
exists to remove.

### 5.4 · The grain problem in your `organelle_label`: ours is set-valued

`organelle_category` is **set-valued** (stated in 008, semicolon-delimited per D9), and so is
`organelle_subcategory` (D14). Your `organelle_label` is a `string`.

It is set-valued because GO is a DAG: a term can descend from two anchors and legitimately belong to
two categories, and a protein can hold several across its terms (D5: *all are reported*). Per
`(group, term)` row it is usually one value — but "usually" is what makes this dangerous rather than
obvious, because it will pass every test you write until the first term that has two.

**Please do not semicolon-join it into `organelle_label`.** A set in a string column is the pairing
hazard D14 exists to prevent, and it would sit right next to a second set (`organelle_subcategory`)
that you would then also have to join, at which point position-pairing is the only way to read either
of them. Either repeat the row per category, or normalize to a child table. Your call — you know your
query patterns — but the string is the one option we would argue against.
