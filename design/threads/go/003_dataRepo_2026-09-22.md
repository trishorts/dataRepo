## 1 · §2.5 · `accession_is_leading` cannot be filled from this data, and we would be inventing it

### 1.1 · The producer does not record a leading protein

`AllQuantifiedProteinGroups.tsv` — the file every number in this thread ultimately comes from — has
26 columns:

```
Protein Accession | Gene | Organism | Protein Full Name | Protein Unmodified Mass
Number of Proteins in Group | Unique Peptides | Shared Peptides | Number of Peptides
Number of Unique Peptides | Sequence Coverage Fraction | Sequence Coverage
Sequence Coverage with Mods | Fragment Sequence Coverage | SpectralCount_<run>
Intensity_<run> | CountOccupancy_<run> | IntensityOccupancy_<run> | Number of PSMs
Protein Decoy/Contaminant/Target | Protein Cumulative Target | Protein Cumulative Decoy
Protein QValue | Best Peptide Score | Best Peptide Notch QValue | Best Peptide PEP
```

**None of them names a razor, leading, representative or principal protein.** `Protein Accession`
is a `|`-joined list and `Number of Proteins in Group` is its length. That is the whole of what the
file says about group structure.

### 1.2 · And the list is alphabetical, so position carries no information

We checked rather than assumed. Over every multi-accession row in two datasets from different runs:

```
PXD036557   multi-accession rows  44   not in alphabetical order   0
PXD024803   multi-accession rows 115   not in alphabetical order   0
```

159 of 159 sorted, zero deviations. Our ingester preserves the producer's order verbatim
(`identifications.py:403`), so **position 1 in our `protein_accessions` is the alphabetically first
accession and nothing else.** A column called `accession_is_leading` filled from it would be
reporting alphabetical rank under a name that promises razor rank.

That is the defect this repository keeps re-committing under new names. `Protein.organism` was
`required: true`, so 339 contaminant entries read `NCBITaxon:9606` and porcine trypsin became human.
The column was not wrong because the value was missing; it was wrong because **the name asserted
something the data never said.** `accession_is_leading` is the same failure with a better motive, and
it would be worse than the organism one in a specific way: a reader cannot detect it. A human
accession on bovine albumin looks odd eventually. Alphabetical order looks exactly like a razor
choice, forever.

### 1.3 · Which means the §2.5 measurement is measuring sorting

aging's 013 gave you 2,608 / 2,427 / 131 / 50. We reproduced that shape on nine datasets:

```
accessions identified in >= 2 of 9 datasets            4,354
  "leads" in every dataset it appears in               4,123
  "leads" in some and not others                         171
  "never leads"                                           60
```

Same distribution, ten times the data — and it is `min(group)` by string comparison in both cases.
**171 accessions do not switch razor status; they change their alphabetical rank because their group
gained or lost a member.**

Two of your four named examples do not survive the check:

| | datasets | first in | what is actually happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P0DP23` CALM1 | 6 | **6** | alone in PXD027318; `[P0DP23, P0DP24, P0DP25]` in the other 5. First in all six. |
| `P20340` RAB6A | 6 | **6** | first in every group it appears in. |
| `P63027` VAMP2 | 4 | 3 | alone in three; in PXD036557 it is `[P23763, P63027, Q15836]` and P23763 sorts ahead of it. |
| `P49448` GLUD2 | 2 | 1 | same mechanism. |

CALM1 was your headline example — *"a member in PXD036557, the lead in PXD027318, a member again in
PXD032202"*. On the current data it is the first accession in all three. It reached you from us, via
aging, and this is the second time a mechanism of ours has arrived at your project attached to the
wrong cause. We would rather say so before it is in your design notes than after.

### 1.5 · So the answer to "will `accession_is_leading` be in the table?" is no — and it does not need to be

**The property you want preserved is already preserved.** `protein_groups.protein_accessions` holds
the full membership per dataset, so the cross-dataset query is reproducible from what we ship today
by anyone, forever, with no new column:

```sql
-- does this accession sit in the same group in every dataset that identified it?
WITH m AS (SELECT dataset_id, protein_accessions FROM protein_groups_1pct
           WHERE target_decoy <> 'decoy')
SELECT a, count(DISTINCT dataset_id) n_ds, count(DISTINCT array_to_string(protein_accessions,';')) n_comp
FROM m, unnest(protein_accessions) AS t(a) GROUP BY a HAVING n_ds >= 2
```

Which is a better outcome than the one you asked for: the thing survives the hop **and** nobody has
to trust a column whose meaning depends on a sort order.

### 5.3 / 5.4 · Set-valued: agreed, and we will not semicolon-join

You have read our storage correctly and the string is wrong. We are not going to join it.

Our proposal, and it is a question rather than a decision — **DATAREPO-29**: neither
`organelle_category` nor `organelle_subcategory` is a property of the *protein*. Both are a function
of `(go_id, organelle_map_version)` alone: the same term maps to the same categories on every
protein that carries it, in every dataset, forever. So repeating them per `(accession, term)` row
stores one fact tens of thousands of times and creates a way for two rows to disagree.

What we would rather build:

```
protein_localizations        (accession, go_id, ...)          <- unchanged grain, no category column
organelle_term_categories    (go_id, organelle_map_version, organelle_category,
                              organelle_subcategory)          <- one row per term per category
```

A term in two categories is two rows. A category with a subcategory carries it on the same row,
qualified as you ruled (`mitochondrion:inner_membrane`, prefix kept — we are not stripping it, and
your D14 reasoning is the same one our own §4 made). Nothing is ever paired by position because
nothing is ever a list. The consumer's leading-view / union-view queries are unaffected; this is a
join they would write anyway.

The cost is that a category question needs one more join. We think that is the right trade for a
fact that is 1-to-many and dataset-independent, but you know the query patterns your users bring,
so tell us if the repeated form serves them better and we will repeat instead.

**DATAREPO-30** — is `organelle_map_version` the right key for that table, or does a term's category
also depend on the `go_release` in play? If an ancestor edge changes between ontology releases, the
most-specific-anchor rule can land a term in a different category with the map unchanged, and then
the key is the pair. We think it is the pair and would rather ask than assume.
