   you measured.
2. **The measurement you asked for (§1): we cannot split it.** aging has deposited SDRFs for 10

3. **What we can say about the community-annotated ones:** mzLib's own informativeness gate rates

## 1 - The ten

The gate is `pymzlib.sdrf.assess`, pyMzLib 0.2.0, which wraps mzLib's `SdrfSampleInformativeness`.
We ran it on every distinct SDRF under aging's run folders: 12 files, 10 distinct by sha256.

| verdict | n | accessions | what the gate saw |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Informative | 1 | PXD060431 | factor varies, samples described, replicates vary |
| Partial | 8 | PXD001839, PXD002467, PXD010115, PXD011314, PXD026608, PXD034432, PXD048658, PXD066430 | samples described, but no factor and one replicate number |
| Skeleton | 1 | PXD036557 | nothing described |

**It agreed with a correction we made today.** Our own ingest had called four of the Partial ones
"no annotation": PXD010115, PXD011314, PXD026608 and PXD034432. Their SDRFs name the tissue (`Blood
serum`, `Blood`, `Urine`) but give no ontology term. We fixed that in datarepo 0.18.0 before running
this, and the gate agrees with the fix: it calls all four "described".

**What "Partial" means for age:**
- None of the ten has a factor that varies with age.
- **PXD060431 is the only one whose samples differ in a factor at all.**

So your conclusion holds on our side too: a deposited SDRF is the only route to per-sample biology,
and on this corpus it rarely goes that far.

**For the comparison you want,** sdrf's corpus is the right place, not ours. The mzLib 1.0.592
release notes report the same gate over the bigbio annotated corpus as 300 informative, 742
partial and 194 skeleton. We have not checked whether that corpus holds any submitter files.
