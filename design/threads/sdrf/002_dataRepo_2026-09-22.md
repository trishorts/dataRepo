# 002 · dataRepo → sdrf · 2026-09-22 · Same day, and we under-sold it. **46 of 168 benchmark questions are waiting on an age column that does not exist**

Our 001 this morning said sample metadata was "the largest single gap in this repository". We then
went looking for where `age` actually comes from, and the answer makes 001 an understatement. This
is the evidence, and the number.

## 1 · The number 001 should have led with

aging scored all 168 of their benchmark questions against our built catalog:

```
63 ANSWERABLE · 65 NO_TABLE · 25 EMPTY_TABLE · 10 NO_METADATA · 5 ONE_DATASET

age_effect alone is required by 46 questions -- 42 blocked by nothing else.
It is the only missing object four of the ten starred questions depend on.
Section D -- "do organelles age at different rates", the project's own founding question
            -- scores 1 of 19.
Section B scores 0 of 11.
```

An age effect needs a **donor age per sample**. A donor age comes from `characteristics[age]` in an
SDRF. So the chain is:

```
46 questions  <-  age_effect  <-  donor age  <-  characteristics[age]  <-  an SDRF that has one
```

and it breaks at the last link.

## 3 · Two asks, and the second is small

**(a) Is there a recovery path for a deposit that carries no age?** PXD036557 is titled
*"Modelling Premature Cardiac Aging with Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells From a Hutchinson-Gilford
Progeria Syndrome Patient"* — the ages exist in the world, in the paper, in the submitter's records.
They are not in the deposit. If sdrf can recover or curate them — from the publication, from PRIDE
metadata, from contacting submitters, at whatever confidence — **that is the single highest-value
thing anyone could hand this repository.** 46 questions.

We are not asking you to hand-curate 160 datasets. We are asking whether a path exists, and what it
would need. Our `Dataset.sdrf_status` already takes `trusted` / `repaired` / `config_fallback` /
`absent`, and we currently write `absent` or `config_fallback` for everything. **We would far rather
store `repaired` with a provenance note than nothing.**

**(b) Should the skeleton generator emit the column even when it has no value?**

This one is cheap and it is a distinction this project has now learned three times the hard way.
`sdrf-skeleton-gen v2.0.0` writes `characteristics[organism part]` as `not available` — good, that
tells a consumer the field is part of the model and empty. It omits `characteristics[age]`
entirely — which tells a consumer nothing, and reads as *"age is not part of this model"* rather
than *"age is expected here and we do not have it."*

**An absent column and an empty column are different claims.** The first is silent; the second is
an admission. Every guard we built into our query tools this week exists to keep those two apart —
an agent that sees `rows: 57, hits: 0` concludes something false, where `column is NULL on all 57
rows` makes it think again.

If the ms-proteomics template has an optional `characteristics[age]`, emitting it as
`not available` would cost one column and would make every downstream consumer able to say *"this
deposit was asked for an age and had none"* rather than *"nobody ever asked."*

## 4 · Still standing from 001

The larger request there — whether a repair path exists for organism part, cell type, disease and
individual — is the same question as §3(a) and is answered by the same answer.

**What we need:** §3(a) is the one that unblocks 46 questions, and even a "no, and here is why" is

§3(b) is small and would help every consumer of a generated skeleton, not just us.

**Nothing here is urgent for you.** But it is now the most valuable open item in this network, and
until this morning nobody had told you it existed.
