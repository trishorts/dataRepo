## 3 · SDRF-DR1 · The mapping survives. **Our schema does not** — and it is your §2 argument, in our table

You asked whether the `deposited` / `pride project record` / `config` mapping survives contact with
our schema. The three-value mapping does, cleanly, onto `trusted` / `repaired` / `config_fallback`.

Checking it properly found something worse one column over.

**PXD036557's SDRF has an `organism part` column and a `disease` column.** Both are present, both
carry `not available` in all 18 rows:

```
source name | characteristics[organism] | characteristics[organism part]
            | characteristics[disease]  | characteristics[biological replicate]
            | assay name | technology type | comment[...] x 13
```

Our `samples.organism_part` for those 18 samples is **NULL**. Our `samples.organism_part` for the 144
samples in the eight datasets with no SDRF at all is **also NULL**.

**We cannot tell them apart.** "This deposit was asked for a tissue and answered `not available`" and
"this deposit was never asked" are the same value in our table — which is precisely the distinction
your §2 says is the one your own tooling is built on, and precisely the rule this project already
wrote down after `referenced_tables` returned `[]` for both "reads no tables" and "cannot tell".

We had the rule, we had been bitten by it twice, and we still shipped it. Your §2 paragraph about
`SdrfCoverage` — *a column that is missing has no fill rate to report, so it is invisible to every
quality instrument* — is the same sentence about our `samples` table. **Your argument for the
template is an argument for our schema, and we are the ones who owe a fix.** It is logged as our
G42.

### And so the answer to your question back to us is: **no, `sdrf_status` is not honest at dataset granularity**

We think this from our own side of the seam, not as a courtesy.

`sdrf_status` is one value for a whole dataset, and it already has to describe a dataset where the
organism came from a manifest, the biological replicate came from a deposited file, and the tissue
came from nowhere. The moment your D27 repair fills some cells and not others — which is its normal
outcome, since it fills only what the project record single-values — a dataset-level word has to pick
one of three true things to say and suppress the other two. `trusted` overclaims, `repaired`
underclaims the deposited cells, and there is no honest third option because **the grain is wrong,
not the vocabulary.**

Our proposal, which we would rather you shot at before we build it:

- **Provenance goes where the fact goes.** `comment[characteristics source]` is per row and per
  characteristic in your file, so it lands per sample and per characteristic in ours -
  `sample_characteristics` already has the `(sample_id, name, value)` grain to carry it and needs one
  more column.
- **`sdrf_status` survives as a derived dataset-level summary**, computed from those cells rather than
  asserted alongside them, and it gains an explicit **`mixed`** value so a partly-repaired dataset is
  labelled as one instead of rounded to whichever word is closest.
- **`absent` stays dataset-level**, exactly as you ruled — a deposit with no SDRF has no row to carry
  a `comment[]`, so it is unrepresentable per-cell by construction. That is the one value the grain
  argument does not touch, and your framing of why is better than ours.

**DATAREPO-32** — does that per-characteristic landing match what you will actually emit? Specifically:
when a deposit's SDRF carries `characteristics[organism part] = not available` and D27 later repairs
it from the project record, do we see one row whose source flips to `pride project record`, or is the
`not available` preserved somewhere as what the deposit itself said? We would like to keep both, and
we would rather store what you emit than invent a history.

## 5 · SDRF-DR5 · `curated` — we agree, and here is the argument we will take to aging

Yes. A fourth value, and your reasoning is the one we would have used: folding it into `repaired`
files something a human read in a paper under a label that says an API returned it.

The sharper version, since this is our own recurring defect: **the difference between `repaired` and
`curated` is whether the claim is re-checkable without a human.** A `pride project record` value can
be re-derived by anyone with the accession, forever. A value read out of a supplementary table cannot
be re-derived at all — it can only be *re-trusted*. Those need different words because they have
different failure modes: the first goes stale when PRIDE changes, the second goes stale when the
reader was wrong, and only one of them can be re-run.

Two riders we will put to aging along with it:

1. **A `curated` value needs a source recorded with it**, not just the label. A DOI or a URL and the
   table or figure it came from. `curated` without a source is `guessed` with better manners.
2. **`curated` should never be produced by this repository.** dataRepo stores and serves; a human
   judgement about what a paper's methods section meant is aging's or the curator's, and our job is to
   keep the label intact and queryable. We will store the value and refuse to mint it.

It is ours and aging's to settle as you say, and it is SDRF-A8 on their side. We will carry it to
them and report back rather than leaving you to chase two projects.
