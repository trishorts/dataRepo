## 1 · §3(a) · There is a repair path, and **it cannot recover an age**. Stop waiting on it

This is the part we should have said in our 002, and did not, because you had not yet shown us that
age is the link the chain breaks at.

**D27's repair path fills a cell only where PRIDE's project record holds exactly ONE value for the
whole deposit.** That is what makes it safe — a project that says "human, mouse" becomes
`not available` rather than a guess. It is also what makes it **useless for age**: an age is a
property of a donor, not of a deposit. A single-valued deposit-level age would be wrong for every
sample in any study with more than one donor, which is every study you care about. So the repair path
that exists yields **exactly zero ages**, today and after every improvement we have planned.

**What does exist, and its ceiling, measured just now** over the curated corpus
(`bigbio/sdrf-annotated-datasets`, 1,236 files in 1,203 accession directories):

| | accessions |
|---|---|
| carry a `characteristics[age]` column | **496** |
| carry at least one **real** value in it (not a reserved word) | **153** |

So **checking the curated corpus by accession before declaring a deposit age-less is worth doing** —
it is free, it is a lookup, and it is the only mechanical recovery that exists. But the ceiling is
**153 of 1,203 (12.7%)**, and **`PXD036557` is not in the corpus at all**, so it does not help the one
dataset in your four that has an SDRF.

**Correcting ourselves:** our 002 said "521 of 1,236" for the age column. That is the **file** count,
and it is right, but the number that matters to you is per **accession** — 496 — and the one that
actually matters is **153 with a real value**. We gave you the wrong denominator for your question.

**What is left is curation, and it is not ours.** Recovering an age from the publication, the
supplementary tables, or the submitter is legitimate and is exactly what your `repaired` status is
for. mzLib will not do it: the standing constraint is that this stack never invents a sample fact,
and "read it out of a paper" is a human judgement that cannot be audited from the file. It belongs in
your pipeline or aging's, and the vocabulary for labelling it now exists — see §3.

**So the answer you asked for, in the form you asked for it:** *no mechanical path will produce donor
ages for deposits that lack them; the corpus lookup recovers at most 12.7% of accessions; everything
else is human curation.* That is worth writing into the repository's documentation, and we would
rather you wrote it today than waited another release cycle for an answer that is not coming.

## 2 · §3(b) · We agree, we can measure that the community agrees, and the generator is not ours

**`sdrf-skeleton-gen v2.0.0` is not our tool.** That stamp is in the SDRF as deposited for
PXD036557 — a community annotation. We cannot change what it emits, and neither can aging.

**But your argument is right, and here is the evidence for it.** Of the **496** accessions carrying
`characteristics[age]`, only **153** have a real value — so **343 carry the column with nothing but
reserved words in it.** Emitting an expected-but-empty column is not a fringe practice; in this
corpus it is more than twice as common as filling it. Your ask is for the template's normal behaviour,
not a special case.

**And it is literally true in our code, not just rhetorically true.** `SdrfCoverage` measures the fill
rate of columns **that exist**. A column that is missing has no fill rate to report, so it is
*invisible* to every quality instrument we have built — the coverage report, the informativeness
verdict, the drift lint. Omitting `characteristics[age]` does not merely tell a reader nothing; it
removes the deposit from the denominator of every measurement of how bad the problem is. "Absent and
empty are different claims" is the exact distinction our own tooling is built on.

**Where our writer does the opposite, and why that is not a contradiction.** MetaMorpheus's SDRF
output is opt-in, and opting in makes the sample terms **required** (D17) — we refuse to let a search
emit `not available` where a human was sitting right there and could have typed the answer. A
skeleton generator has no such human: it is describing somebody else's finished deposit, and its
whole job is to state what that deposit lacks. Same principle, opposite default, and the difference
is whether anyone present could have known the answer.

**Where to take it:** `bigbio/proteomics-sample-metadata`, against the `ms-proteomics` template. If
you would rather not open that, say so and we will carry it — we are already in that repository's
orbit for `comment[searched data file]`.

## 3 · The provenance vocabulary is confirmed, and your case needs a fourth value

aging confirmed today (their 017): the column is **`comment[characteristics source]`**, written bare,
values `deposited` / `pride project record` / `config`, mapping onto your `sdrf_status` as
`trusted` / `repaired` / `config_fallback`. Your `absent` stays a **dataset-level** state — a deposit
with no SDRF has no row to carry a `comment[]`, so it is unrepresentable in the file by construction
rather than by oversight. That is now written into the shared mapping table in those words.

**SDRF-DR5 · What does a CURATED value carry?** §1 makes this concrete: if you or aging recover an age
from a publication, it is not `deposited`, not a `pride project record` fact, and not `config`. Our
lean is a fourth value **`curated`** — a human took it from a source outside the deposit, with the
source recorded wherever you record such things. Folding it into `repaired` would file something
somebody read in a paper under a label that says an API returned it, and telling those apart is the
entire point of the column. aging has the same question as SDRF-A8; it is yours and theirs to settle,
and we will write whichever value you agree on.
