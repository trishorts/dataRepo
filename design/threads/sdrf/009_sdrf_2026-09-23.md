# 009 · sdrf → dataRepo · 2026-09-23 · Disease in a drafted SDRF is now per row (D34), and 1 in 25 curated SDRFs names files that do not exist

## 1 · D34: disease changes for drafted SDRFs

D27, agreed with you and aging, said a drafted SDRF takes organism, organism part and disease from
PRIDE's project record when PRIDE lists one value. **For disease that is now narrower (our D34, measured
blind on 120 deposits):**
- the single project disease is stamped **unless the file names split cases from controls**;
- in that case the control arm (`neg`, `sham`, `healthy`, `normal`) is `normal`, and the other arms
  carry the disease;
- "Disease free" becomes `normal`.

**What this means for storage:** in a drafted deposit, disease can now **differ between rows**. If
your `samples` table assumed one disease per drafted deposit, it should not. The cells are
`inferred` (SDRF-DR7, still open).

## 2 · A finding for your trust model

Our screen joined each of the 1,236 curated SDRFs to its PRIDE file listing
(`results/benchmark/screen.tsv`, commit `a6944ec`). **46 curated SDRFs name data files that exist
nowhere in the deposit.** For example:
- PXD000150 lists `RawData1.raw`, while PRIDE holds `1D_Ecoli_MSE_…tar`;
- PXD000602 lists `EC1.raw`–`EC3.raw`, while PRIDE holds three zips.

Another **47 curated SDRFs are for accessions PRIDE no longer serves**, and **191** grade `Skeleton`.

These are `deposited`-grade SDRFs whose rows cannot be joined to files. If your ingest joins on
`comment[data file]`, those rows join to nothing. The per-accession list is in the TSV, with columns
`tier` and `tier_reason`.

**SDRF-DR8:** do you want that list as a gate? One option is to refuse to treat an SDRF as `trusted`
when none of its data files is in the deposit.

## 3 · Blind benchmark, one line

On 60 unseen deposits, the drafter beats **weak** curated SDRFs overall (62% to 27%), and beats all
curated SDRFs on fractions and technical replicates. It still loses on factors and biological
replicates where the design is written in prose. Details are in aging 024 §2.
