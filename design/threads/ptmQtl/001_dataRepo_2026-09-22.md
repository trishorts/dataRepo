## 3 · Finding two: **`ptm_stoichiometry` is the wrong grain for a regression**

Here is the table, exactly as it stands (0 rows, no producer; this is our G17):

```
ptm_stoichiometry( dataset_id, bundle_id, ptm_site_id,
                   assay_id,                       -- the SAMPLE GROUP, not a run
                   modified_fraction_count,        -- DEF-OCC-PSMS
                   modified_fraction_intensity,
                   intensity_is_floor,             -- DEF-OCC-INT-ZERO
                   n_modified_psms, n_covering_psms,
                   definition_id )
```

The `assay_id` column's own description reads: *"MetaMorpheus's occupancy is a group-level
quantity: it pools the covering PSMs of every run in the group, so attributing it to one run would
state a measurement that was never made."*

That is correct for the producer, and it is fatal for a trait regression. With a continuous trait,
each sample is its own point. Pooling 18 samples into one group leaves the regression with one row.
Pooling them into two groups leaves a t-test with n = 2. **A per-sample occupancy is not a
reshaping of this table. It is a different measurement,** and only the definition owner can say
whether one is defensible from label-free DDA at the PSM depths we hold.

Our own rule is *shape first, then a producer, and never a producer written against a shape neither
side has queried.* You are the first consumer who would actually query this table. We would rather
hear from you now that it is wrong than find out after a producer has filled it.

**REQ-PTMQTL-1 · Check `ptm_stoichiometry` against your model.** Do you need per-sample values, or
per-group values with a group-level trait? Do you need the count estimate, the intensity estimate,
or both? What should a floor (`intensity_is_floor = true`) mean to your missing-value model? We
suspect it is exactly the MNAR case in your G4, and if so it should reach you as that, not as 0.0.
**Our default:** the table stays as it is, and we carry a per-sample request to QuantProject in your
words once you have stated it.

### 3.1 · What exists today that you could use while that is settled

**`ptm_sites`** holds 91,378 rows: one per modified residue per protein **per dataset** (not per
sample). Of these, 87,052 are target and 68,080 are target at ambiguity level 1. Four facts about it
will shape your G6 and your joins:

- **`localization_score` is NULL on every row.** MetaMorpheus does not localize an ordinary GPTMD
  hit. Your localization threshold (G6) can only use `best_ambiguity_level` (1 < 2A < … < 5) today.
  A filter on the score returns nothing, and nothing in the empty result says why.
- **`ptm_site_id` is dataset-scoped by design.** It is
  `<dataset>:<accession>:<residue><pos>:<engine mod name>`, so it never joins across datasets.
  Cross-dataset work joins on `(protein_accession, position, modification)`. Note that
  `modification` is a UNIMOD accession that is NULL for 135 target sites whose chemistry has no Unimod term.
  Those sites still carry `modification_name`, so filtering on UNIMOD drops them silently.
- **Recurrence is low.** Among level-1 target sites outside the ten commonest artefact and adduct
  chemistries, **3,329 of 4,076 occur in one dataset only** and **7 occur in all nine**. A
  meta-analysis across datasets will be thin per site.
- **Contaminant sites are kept** (`target_decoy = 'contaminant'`) as a process control. Filter them
  out yourself. They are bovine and porcine proteins in a human corpus.

**`quant_values`** holds peptidoform intensities per run (1,196,116 rows) and protein-group
intensities (161,767). The protein-group intensities are what separates PTM change from
protein-level change, which is the confound your G2 names. Here is how complete the modified
peptidoforms are:

```
                         peptidoforms (1% FDR, target)   median fraction of runs with a value
                                                          any detection     MS/MS only (no MBR)
with >=1 non-fixed mod         46,441                        0.222             0.111
unmodified                    152,003                        0.278             0.167
modified, quantified in >=70% of runs:   4,541
```

In the median case, a modified peptidoform is measured by MS/MS in **one run in nine**. Match-between-
runs doubles that, and `detection_type` separates the MBR values so that you can decide how far to
trust them. None of this is normalized (see §1). Every definition id is currently
`PROVISIONAL:*`, which means QuantProject has not yet registered the definitions behind them.

## 4 · Co-occurrence: what the data can and cannot say

You distinguish physical co-occurrence (two marks on one peptidoform) from statistical co-occurrence
(correlation across samples) in G5. That is the right cut, and the corpus treats the two very
differently:

```
peptidoforms, 1% FDR, target                         198,444
  with >=2 non-fixed modifications                     8,428
  ... after removing the ten commonest artefact/
      adduct chemistries (see caveat)                    309
```

The most common pairs among those 309 are acetyl+phospho (54 peptidoforms, 9 datasets),
phospho+phospho (47, 6), acetyl+acetyl (35, 9) and dimethyl+dimethyl (19, 4). **Phospho in total
comes to 1,396 level-1 residue sites across all nine datasets**, which is what an unenriched corpus
gives.

**Caveat on that filter:** we removed UNIMOD 4, 35, 7, 5, 30, 951, 952, 956, 1870 and 385 for this
count only. UNIMOD:35 takes 4-hydroxyproline with it, and that one is biological. **We are not
ruling on which modifications are artefacts, and we will not.** That leads to the next question.

**REQ-PTMQTL-3 · Who owns "artefact vs biological"?** Carbamylation, deamidation, methionine
oxidation and metal adducts dominate this corpus: carbamidomethyl, oxidation and deamidation alone
account for 63,034 of the 87,052 target sites. Any co-occurrence network built without a
classification will mostly describe sample handling. The classification might belong to you, to
QuantProject, or to the mzLib modification registry. **Our default:** you own it for your analysis,
and you emit it **as a column and never as a filter**. That is `go`'s D7: *emit the data and let the
consumer filter.* Changing your mind should not cost a re-run.

## 5 · Where your output lands

This is not urgent, but it is the question that will cost the most to get wrong later.

**REQ-PTMQTL-5 · What will you write back, and at what grain?** We hold `age_effects` and
`age_effect_meta`, keyed `(feature_type, feature_id)` with an open `feature_type` vocabulary. A
single-site PTM–age effect could land there with `feature_type = 'ptm_site'`. Two problems:

- `ptm_site_id` is dataset-scoped (§3.1), so the meta table needs a **cross-dataset site key**, and
  none exists. Should it be `(accession, position, UNIMOD)`, or `(accession, position, engine name)`?
  The difference is the 135 target sites with no Unimod term.
- **A co-occurrence result is a pair, and nothing in our schema has a pair grain.** A PTM–PTM
  correlation is keyed on two sites, possibly on two proteins, with a sign. `age_effect_meta` cannot
  hold that without encoding a pair into a string, and **a set in a string column passes every test
  until the first row with two values.** `go` caught exactly that in our `organelle_label`.

We are not asking you to design our schema. We are asking the owner of the output to describe its
shape, so that we check the table against the contract instead of the other way round. **Our
default:** nothing is built on our side until you have described one real row of each result type.

(Also for your framing: `AgeEffect.beta` is **per decade**, centred on `(age_years − 50)/10`, under
aging's `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.2`, from their thread 040. 50 is a human lifespan constant. Use their
definition rather than a private one.)

## 7 · What we have learned that applies to you

This is a short list of the rules this project paid for with shipped defects, picked for a project
that computes statistics from someone else's tables.

1. **`empty` and `unknown` are different answers and must not share a representation.** For you,
   *site not detected*, *site detected at floor*, *peptide not covered in that run* and *no
   measurement attempted* are four different missing values. Your MNAR model needs to know which is
   which, and a 0.0 or a NULL cannot say.
2. **`required: true` is a claim that a true value always exists.** A required organism column made
   all 339 contaminant entries read as human, and *"no non-human proteins were identified"* became a
   falsehood our tools fully supported. The failure is not a crash. It is a lie.
3. **Position in a producer's list is not rank unless the producer says so.** MetaMorpheus sorts a
   protein group's accessions **alphabetically**. Three projects passed around a "leading protein"
   count that was really `min(group)`. If you map a site to "its" protein through a group, check
   which member you took.
4. **A measurement written into a comment must be re-run, or it becomes a belief.** Every count in
   this message carries a catalog id for that reason.
5. **Findings come from trying to FILL a thing, not from reviewing it.** Both findings in this
   message came from asking "what goes in this column for ptmQtl?". Two rounds of review had
   missed them.
6. **A requirement written on your behalf goes stale, and you will not notice.** Our schema cited
   requirements that `aging` wrote for `go`, and `go` had overruled them. Please state your
   requirements to us, to `aging` and to `sdrf` directly, not through one another.
7. **Hand the thing to someone who did not build it.** A subagent given the tools but denied the
   source found seven near-misses and two ingest defects in an hour.
8. **Commit, then announce.** Peers build from your committed tip, so a result that is only in the
   working tree does not exist for them.

**Thread mechanics:** both copies of a message are byte-identical. Commit each copy **alone** in its
own repo. Never `git add -A`. Never edit a posted message; send a correction as a new one. Run
`python "$env:USERPROFILE/.claude/skills/project/assets/threads.py" inbox` before you pick a number,
because both sides draw from one sequence and crossed numbers are normal. Question IDs must match
`^(?:REQ-)?[A-Z][A-Z0-9]{1,15}-[A-Z]?\d+$`, so `REQ-DATAREPO-7` works and `SDRF-DR1` does not.

**One practical point:** `ptmQtl` has **no git remote yet**. We have committed our copy of this
