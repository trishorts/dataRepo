## 1 - DATAREPO-P1: `trait_effects`, yes, as a new generic table. `age_effects` stays

You are right that a generic core should not name a table after one consumer's trait, and
`trait_effects` is in the **core** schema, which knows nothing about aging. But `age_effects` is not
ours to fold into it:

- **It lives in aging's study layer**, which is the place built for consumer-specific tables. A
  study layer adds tables and never alters the core.
- **It has a different producer and a different content.** Its rows are aging's own protein-level
  fits under `aging:DEF-AGE-EFFECT`, with columns forced by their benchmark (`estimator`,
  `quant_basis`, `model_form`, `age_span_years`, a refusals table). Your rows are peptidoform
  hurdle fits under `ptmQtl:DEF-PTM-TRAIT-HURDLE`. The columns do not line up, and merging them would
  give us one table with two meanings in its NULLs.
- **Your aging rows go in `trait_effects`** with `trait_id = aging:DEF-AGE-EFFECT v1.2`, as you
  proposed. If aging later wants its own fits moved over, that is their call, and nothing blocks it.

**Your columns, with four changes:**

| yours | ours | why |
|---|---|---|
| `protein_group` (`P12345;P12346`) | `protein_accessions`, a list | Never a joined string. Your own note says to carry all accessions, and a list carries them with no separator rule. |
| `feature_type` values `occupancy_site` | FeatureType `ptm_site` | It is the same thing. Our enum already has it. |
| (none) | NULL rule stated on the table | `beta_*`/`se_*`/`p_*` are nullable **exactly when** `estimable_* = false`. The flag is the answer. The NULL carries nothing. |
| key | `(feature_type, feature_key, trait_id, response, scope, definition_id)` | Declared now, while the table is empty. A duplicated source row is refused at build time. |

`n_detected`, `n_absent_protein_present`, `n_protein_present`, `estimable_*`, `mbr_dependent`,
`mod_class`, `fdr_family` and `definition_id` are **required**. Each always has a true value, and
required is a claim that it does. `feature_key_unimod` is nullable. Tell us if it is always
present and we will tighten it.

## 2 - DATAREPO-P2: `ptm_pairs`, yes

Named `ptm_pairs`, because every table name here is plural. Each side is a `feature_key_*` plus a
`protein_accessions_*` list. `flags` and `result_type` are enums with your values and your
definitions. Two additions:

- **`scope`, required.** Your pair row had none, but `trait_effects` has one, and a pair fitted
  within PXD036557 must not share a key with the same pair from the meta-analysis. If pairs are
  only ever computed at one scope, say which and we will drop it. **DATAREPO-P5:** do pairs have a
  scope, and is it the same `<dataset>` / `meta` as `trait_effects`?
- **The key is `(result_type, scope, feature_key_a, feature_key_b, trait_id, stratum, statistic,
  definition_id)`.** **DATAREPO-P6:** is a pair ordered? If `(a, b)` and `(b, a)` can both be
  written, the key treats them as two rows. We would rather you tell us a canonical order, for
  example `a < b` by string, than find out from a duplicate.

## 3 - DATAREPO-P3: the ProForma key

- **`peptidoforms.peptidoform` is the UNIMOD-resolved ProForma**, and `peptidoform_id` is
  `<dataset>:<that>`. This is your `feature_key_unimod`, and the join into everything we store.
  A modification with no UNIMOD accession is written as a mass tag (`[+37.946941]`), or as
  `[Info:<name>]` when even the mass is unknown. Your 135 no-UNIMOD sites have a key, but it is
  not always the name.
- **New in 0.16.0: `peptidoforms.engine_full_sequences`**, the producer's own string verbatim
  (`KPVADYFL-[UniProt:Leucine methyl ester on L]`). It is a set, because two engine names that
  resolve to one UNIMOD accession make one peptidoform from two strings. It is **not** ProForma,
  because engine names are not a ProForma vocabulary.
- **We do not synthesize "ProForma with engine names".** mzLib is about to do that conversion
  in its reader: our PR D, smith-chem-wisc/mzLib#1346, computes `ProForma` from `Full Sequence`
  when the column is absent.
- **Warning: mzLib's string and ours are NOT byte-identical today**, so do not assume a key from
  one joins to the other. Measured on PR D's test cases:
  - `Calcium on D` is `[UNIMOD:951]` in mzLib and a mass tag in ours;
  - a modification mzLib does not load (UniProt names, extra MetaMorpheus mods) stays named in
    mzLib, `[Common Artifact:Leucine methyl ester on L]`, where ours resolves it from the
    MetaMorpheus install's own list to `[UNIMOD:34]`;
  - an unresolved modification is `[Category:Name on X]` in mzLib and `[Info:...]` in ours.

  Until one converter serves both of us, **join on `engine_full_sequences`**, which is the
  producer's own string and the same whoever reads it. Replacing our translator with mzLib's
  would re-identify peptidoforms, and we will say so on this thread before it happens.

**Caveat:** a peptidoform ingested before 0.16.0 has no `engine_full_sequences`. aging's
re-ingest on `c619612` fills it.

**How exact it is.** The catalog stores UNIMOD ProForma, which does not keep the engine category

## 5 - Your `mod_class` default drops most phosphosites

In aging's MetaMorpheus output, **phosphoserine appears as `UniProt:Phosphoserine` 33,092 times and
as `Common Biological:Phosphorylation on S` 1,765 times.** Methyl-R, acetyl-K, citrulline and
hydroxy-P split the same way. MetaMorpheus names a modification with the UniProt entry's own name
when the database annotates it, and with the GPTMD name otherwise. So one chemistry lands in two
categories.

mzLib's `ModificationType` for those entries is `UniProt`. A default of "`Common Biological` means
biological" would class about 95% of phosphoserine as non-biological. That is why §4 counts
(ii). Under (i) the four exact datasets give 860 fittable, against 2,168 under (ii).

It gets worse at the other end. `N[UNIMOD:7]` is both `Common Artifact` deamidation and UniProt
"Deamidated asparagine", so a category-to-class map is **many-to-many**. It needs a per-chemistry
rule, not a per-category one. That is your call and aging's (their L1). We report it because we
counted it.
