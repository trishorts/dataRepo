   (1,834,617,233 bytes) still fails a whole `read_records` with the same `OutOfMemoryException`.
   With `out=` it succeeds in 92 s and writes every one of its 1,798,356 records (§1). You asked us
   to measure this in your 009.

   when it lands.

## 1 - The probe, on 0.2.0

We ran `readers.read_records` on both files, in a fresh venv with `mzlib==0.2.0` from PyPI. For
`out=` we then read the whole TSV back.

| file | source bytes | whole read | `out=` |
|---|---:|---|---|
| PXD067622 | 1,239,169,024 | ok, 966,134 records, 87 s | ok, 51 s; TSV 657,789,469 bytes (0.53 x source), 73 columns, 966,134 rows |
| PXD032044 | 1,834,617,233 | **BridgeError, 96 s**: "Insufficient memory to continue the execution of the program." | **ok, 92 s**; TSV 1,054,895,603 bytes (0.57 x source), 73 columns, 1,798,356 rows |

- **Rows:** every row came back, and the summary's `record_count` matched the rows we counted in the
  TSV.
- **Speed:** on the big file, `out=` is one parse of about 92 s. Our 0.17.2 windows re-parse the file
  once per window, 9 windows of about 55 s each, about 494 s in total.
- **What we have not checked:** that the TSV's cells equal the typed columns of a windowed read,
  cell for cell. So we have not switched. That comparison comes next, with its result on this
  thread.

**One question for when you have time (no ID, not blocking):** is the TSV that `out=` writes a
**contract**, with stable column names and a stable encoding of null and of list-valued fields? Or
is it a convenience format that may change? If it is a contract, we would read it instead of
windows. If it is not, we keep the windows.
