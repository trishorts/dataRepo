## 1 · The cause, read out of your own file

**MetaMorpheus collapses a column to ONE entry when every protein on the row shares it**, while
`Accession` keeps all of them. From `AllPeptides.psmtsv`, PXD032202:

```
Accession     = P60709|P63261         (2 entries)
Organism Name = Homo sapiens          (1 entry -- collapsed, not missing)

alignment over 60,000 rows:
  equal              57,925
  2 accs / 1 org      1,488
  3 accs / 1 org        349
  4 accs / 1 org        104      ... 2,062 rows in total
```

Our reader zipped the two columns **positionally**. The first accession got its species; every
later one indexed past the end of the list and got an empty string. That is the whole defect.

So your three numbers were all ours:

- **442 contaminants with NULL `organism`** — deliberate and correct, a contaminant panel is
  bovine and porcine by design.
- **41,510 NULL non-contaminants** — the ones you could not explain. They are **decoys**;
  `source_db = 'decoy'`, NULL by design, since a reversed sequence is no organism's protein.
- **"zero rows carrying a non-human organism"** — true, because `organism` holds NCBITaxon CURIEs
  and we never assign a non-human one. **The species was in `organism_name`, a column schema 0.0.7
  added and our 034 never mentioned** — and even there, 2,678 uniprot proteins, 4,523 decoys and 9
  contaminants had lost it to the positional zip.

Your hypothesis — a name-to-taxon resolver that knows only *Homo sapiens* — was a reasonable read
of the shape. There is no resolver at all, deliberately (D1, G36): a lookup that knows one species
and defaults the rest is exactly what labelled trypsin human in the first place.

## 2 · What the fix does, including the case it refuses to guess

`_per_accession` takes three branches:

| producer wrote | we do |
|---|---|
| counts match | zip positionally — what the producer means |
| one entry, many accessions | **broadcast it** — they collapsed it *because* the values agree |
| anything else (3 accessions, 2 genes) | **NULL for all of them** |

The third is the one worth arguing about, so here is the argument. When the counts disagree in any
other pattern the alignment is genuinely unknown, and a positional read would put a real gene
symbol on the wrong protein. **A wrong gene reads as a fact; a null reads as "not recorded".** Only
the null cannot be quoted back at someone as evidence. It costs us ~62 rows in 60,000.

`Gene Name` collapses the same way and had the same bug, and so did `add_group_proteins`, where a
short gene list could hand one protein's symbol to another.

## 4 · How this was found, because the method matters more than the bug

Not by a test. Not by either round of agent review — we ran two, and both missed it.

Our first draft of this message said: *"7 contaminant accessions carry no species at all, because
MetaMorpheus wrote none for them."* The user then asked us to open a GitHub issue against
MetaMorpheus about it. Writing that issue meant asserting something about **another project's
output**, and that is not a claim to make from our own parse of their file. So we read their file.

Their file had the species. `A2I7N2` = `Bos taurus`, plainly, in the column we were about to report
as empty. A public issue was one step from being filed, under the user's name, blaming your
upstream for a defect we introduced the day before.

> **A claim about someone else's output is verified at the source, not from your own reading of it.**

Which is the same lesson as our 034 failure, pointed the other way — and both of them are the shape
your §2 named: **a rule shipped without its scope, a column shipped without its name, and a parse
trusted without its source.** We are three for three this week.
