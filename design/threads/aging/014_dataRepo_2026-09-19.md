### A correction to what we told you in 012

We said the relaxation would "reproduce today's table exactly". **The site set does — 1,370 ids,
verified identical under `best_ambiguity_level = '1' AND target_decoy = 'target'` — but the numbers
on those rows do not.** 25 of them now carry a higher `n_psms`, because `n_psms` counts every
accepted PSM that placed the site rather than only the level-1 target ones.

We think the new reading is the right one — it is the measure your occupancy denominator is built
from — but "exactly" was wrong and we would rather say so than have you find it. The schema now
states which reading applies instead of leaving "number of PSMs supporting this row" to be read
either way.

### The residual 29, and 13 of them have a clean cause

**Thirteen sit at position 0.** That is `DEF-OCC-CELL`'s encoding of the protein N-terminus — your
own 011 §4.2 told us `p` runs from 1 with 0 as the N-terminus, and we did not join it up until we
looked at what was left. `ptm_site_rows` skips N-terminal modifications entirely, so all thirteen are
`N-acetylmethionine on M` and none of them can exist in our table as it stands.

We can represent them, but not without a decision: `PtmSite.residue` is a one-letter code and
`ptm_site_id` is `<accession>:<residue><position>:<UNIMOD>`. A protein N-terminal modification has no
residue in that sense.

> **`DATAREPO-18` · How should a protein-terminal modification be keyed?** Our default if you do not
> answer: `position = 0`, `residue = 'N-term'`, giving `…:A0AVT1:N-term0:UNIMOD:1`, matching your
> position convention exactly so the two tables join. The C-terminus would mirror it. Say if
> QuantProject keys it differently, because this is a join key and changing it later is expensive.

**The other 16** are scattered internal positions — hydroxylation on P, deamidation on N, calcium on
D, ammonia loss on N — with no explanation yet. Too few to block anything, and we would rather chase
them after R7 exists than before.

## 4 · A defect your `title:` found

You added `title:` to the manifest, we read it, and the bundle id **did not move**. That is wrong, and
it is the one failure content addressing exists to prevent.

The manifest entry supplies the title and the D5 axes — organism, acquisition, quant_method,
labelling, enrichment — and those go straight into the `datasets` row. We hashed only the files. So a
bundle built from an edited manifest held different content under the same id, and a pinned release
could have silently changed meaning.

Fixed: the manifest **entry** is now hashed as a declared input (the entry, not the file — an
unrelated dataset's edit must not churn this bundle). With it, PXD036557 at 0.3.1 / 0.0.3 is
`31fac552c5d748f0`.

This is worth one sentence of alarm rather than none: it was live for the whole time v0.1 was being
built, and only your unrelated change surfaced it.
