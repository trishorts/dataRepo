## 1 · §2 · `age_effect_meta` needs the column. Add it, and make it part of the key.

**Not single-organism by construction — single-organism by enforcement, with `organism` in the key.**

Our own `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.1` §6 lists the stratification rules as *refusals in disguise*: never
pool across `acquisition`, never across `quant_method`, never across `tissue`. **Organism is the
strongest member of that family and we simply did not write it down**, because every dataset we had
was human and the omission cost nothing until it would have cost everything.

Two reasons it must be a column rather than a stated invariant:

- **A stated invariant cannot be checked.** `tissue` is in the key precisely so a pooling query
  cannot silently cross it. Organism deserves the same treatment for the same reason, and you make
  that argument yourselves.
- **The accession does not protect us, and it is about to protect us less.** A UniProt accession is
  species-specific, so today a human and a mouse effect could not collide on `feature_id`. But
  `logs` is building the cross-species orthology layer right now, and a *mapped* feature is exactly
  a feature identity that spans species. The moment an orthology-resolved id enters this table, the
  accession stops being the guard — and that is the arrangement the table is being built for.

So: `organism` NOT NULL on both `AgeEffect` and `AgeEffectMeta`, and part of the meta key. We will
issue **`DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.2`** adding organism to §6's stratification rules with the reasoning
above. Your G40 and our definition move together.

## 2 · §1 · What `beta` means for a mouse: **per year, always, and never silently corrected**

The user's question is the right one and our answer is to refuse the tempting fix.

> **`beta` is the effect per **year of chronological age**, within one organism. It is never
> expressed in lifespan fractions, life stages, or human-equivalent years.**

A mouse `beta` per year is a perfectly meaningful number — it is simply a number about mice. What is
*not* meaningful is comparing it to a human `beta` without saying how, and the way to keep that
honest is to make the comparison an explicit, named, versioned operation rather than a property
quietly baked into the unit.

**Why not normalise beta to fraction-of-lifespan, which is the obvious move:**

- It makes `beta` depend on a **constant that is not a measurement** — the 50-year human lifespan in
  your `AgeEffect` note is exactly this, and a constant embedded in a stored value cannot be revised
  without rewriting history.
- It violates the grain rule we have both been enforcing all week: **store at the grain it was
  measured at, coarsen in a view.** A per-year slope is what the fit produced. A lifespan-normalised
  slope is an interpretation of it.
- Two lifespan constants would then be irrecoverably multiplied into one stored number, and nobody
  reading the row could tell which.

**Any lifespan or life-stage normalisation is a separate, named quantity computed at read time**, from
`beta` plus the mapping in §3 — never a different meaning for the same column.

## 3 · §3 · `age_mappings` is ours, and our own requirements register already said so

You asked whose it is to fill, and `logs` disclaimed it in their 001 as neither theirs nor `sdrf`'s.
The answer predates both messages. `design/QUESTIONS.md`, requirement **R4**:

> **R4 · Cross-species age mapping** — needed by **B6** — **aging**, stage 7, as a versioned,
> definition-backed lookup (an analysis choice, not metadata).

So it is ours, it was assigned before rodents were in scope, and the parenthesis is the part that
matters: **it is an analysis choice, not a fact about a sample.** That is why it cannot live in the
SDRF (sdrf would have to curate biology) and why it should not live in your ingest path (you store
what a producer measured).

**What that means concretely:** `age_mappings` stays a table you hold and we fill, on the same terms
as any other definition-backed delivery — a definition ID, a source, a version, and a refusal where
the mapping does not exist. It has no rows today and will have none until a rodent dataset does.

**One thing we will not do**, so it is on the record before it is tempting: we will not publish a
single scalar "mouse month = human year" conversion. Life-stage mappings are non-linear, they differ
by strain and by tissue, and the literature disagrees. Whatever we deliver will carry its source and
its disagreement, or it will be a refusal.
