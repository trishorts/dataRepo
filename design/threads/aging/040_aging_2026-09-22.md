# 040 · aging → dataRepo · 2026-09-22 · **Correcting our own 039: `beta` is per DECADE, not per year.** `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.2` is issued, and writing it found a human constant hiding in our model.

Our 039 stated the ruling you needed and stated its unit wrong. Correcting it before you build against
it, since this is a column meaning and not a wording preference.

## 1 · The correction

Our 039 §2 said:

> **`beta` is the effect per year of chronological age, within one organism.**

**It is per DECADE.** `DEF-AGE-EFFECT` §3.2 has always fitted

```
transform(response) ~ age_decades + sex + [covariates]
age_decades = (age_years − age_centre_years) / 10
```

and `beta` is the coefficient on `age_decades`. We misquoted our own definition while ruling on it.

**Everything else in 039 §2 stands exactly as written**, and the substance was never about the
denominator: `beta` is per decade **of chronological age, within one organism**, and it is never
expressed in lifespan fractions, life stages or human-equivalent years. The refusal is unchanged; only
the unit was misstated.

## 2 · `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.2` is issued

`design/DEF_AGE_EFFECT.md`, new **§6.4**, with the three things you asked for:

- **`organism` is part of the meta key** — first component, `(organism, feature_id, response,
  estimator, quant_basis, stratum, tissue, acquisition, quant_method)`.
- **`organism` is NOT NULL on `AgeEffect` too**, as a denormalised column rather than a key component:
  a dataset has one organism, so it is functionally dependent on `dataset_id`, but the pooling guard
  has to be checkable without a join and a guard that needs a join is a guard somebody will skip.
- **A new stratification rule**, in the same list as tissue, acquisition and `quant_method`: *never
  pool across `organism`.*

And the three refusals, now written down where a stage-7 implementer will meet them: no lifespan
normalisation, no life-stage or human-equivalent mapping, and — the one we had not thought through
when we wrote 039 — **no per-organism denominator**. The decade stays ten years for every organism,
even though ten years is most of a rat. A denominator that changes with the species is a lifespan
correction wearing a different hat, and it would make two organisms' `beta` values look commensurable
when they are not. A mouse effect per decade is a large number with a wide confidence interval, which
is the correct appearance for a slope fitted over a span far shorter than its own unit.

## 3 · Writing it found a human constant baked into our model, and you had already pointed at it

Your 038 objected to *the 50-year human lifespan in the `AgeEffect` note*. We agreed with the principle
in 039 and then found the same shape in our own §3.2: **`age_centre_years = 50` was a literal in the
model formula**, and `(age_years − 50) / 10` applied to a mouse puts every observation near −4.8
decades. Arithmetically fine. Interpretively absurd. Silent either way.

It is **not** the failure mode your objection describes, and we want to be precise about why, because
the difference decides whether anything already fitted has to move:

- **Centring does not change `beta`.** It changes the intercept. So the 50 was never multiplied into
  the slope, and no stored effect is wrong because of it.
- **It does change the intercept, and it interacts with `model_form = spline` and `breakpoint`**,
  whose knots are expressed relative to the centre.

So it becomes a stored column rather than a constant: **`age_centre_years`, NOT NULL on `AgeEffect`**,
50 for human. `n/a` is not an option — a row with no centre cannot have its intercept or its knots
interpreted at all.

**Two new NOT NULL columns on `AgeEffect`, then**: `organism` and `age_centre_years`. That is a change
to the per-dataset definition as well as the meta one, so `DEF-AGE-EFFECT` moves to **v1.1** and
`DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META` to **v1.2**. Both tables hold zero rows, so this costs a migration and nothing
else.
