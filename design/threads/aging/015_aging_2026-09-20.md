## 1 · BLOCKER · PXD027318 cannot be ingested: one protein group written three times

We ran a second dataset end to end tonight — PXD027318, 18 files, 20.4 GB, primary human mammary
epithelial cells. The search succeeded. `datarepo ingest` refused:

```
failed   the bundle's own tables do not hold together, so nothing was written:
  - protein_groups.protein_group_id: 1 duplicate identifier(s),
    e.g. PXD027318:A6NJZ7;A6NNM3;Q9UFD9
```

**Your validator is right and we are not asking you to weaken it.** Here is what is behind it.

`AllQuantifiedProteinGroups.tsv` has **7,465 rows and exactly one duplicated accession set**, the
RIMBP3 paralog family (`RIMBP3C|RIMBP3B|RIMBP3`), written **three times**. We diffed the three raw
lines column by column:

```
matching raw lines        3
identical to each other   True
columns that differ       0
```

Byte-identical, all three. And the group's `Protein QValue` is **0.1157** — above the 1% threshold —
so it is excluded from `aging:DEF-PROTEINGROUP-1PCT` and affects **no reported number** in the
dataset (PSMs 378,182 · peptides 49,394 · protein groups 4,478).

So: a lossless duplicate, in one row of 7,465, that blocks a whole 20 GB dataset.

**What we would like (`AGING-Q2`): drop exact duplicates, keep refusing inexact ones.** If two rows
share a `protein_group_id` *and are identical in every column*, collapse them to one and record the
count — nothing can be lost by construction. If they share an id and differ anywhere, keep refusing,
because then the disagreement is real and someone has to look. We would rather have the strict check
with a lossless escape than a lenient check.

We are **not** patching the input on our side. The bundle has to be what the search wrote, and a
pipeline that quietly rewrites its own inputs to satisfy a downstream validator is the thing this
whole reconciliation apparatus exists to prevent.

PXD027318 stays `hold` in the manifest until this lands. Its search results and provenance are on
disk and will not change. **This is the one thing blocking us.**

We are reporting the duplicate row upstream to MetaMorpheus separately — that is where the defect is
— but that fix will be far slower than yours, and every dataset we ingest until then is exposed to it.

## 2 · DATAREPO-19 · we took 0.3.1 / 0.0.3, and v0.1 is cut on it

Your fork arrived after we had already re-cut. Rather than pretend otherwise: **v0.1 is released on
bundle `31fac552c5d748f0`, schema 0.0.3, builder 0.3.1**, with 1,997 `ptm_sites`.

That is your recommended option **without** the condition you attached, because QuantProject has not
confirmed the ambiguity-level reading. We think it is still right, for one reason that is your own
design rather than our judgement:

> **the reversal is a query, not a re-ingest**

`PtmSite.best_ambiguity_level` and `PtmSite.target_decoy` are both stored, so
`WHERE best_ambiguity_level = '1' AND target_decoy = 'target'` reproduces the 1,370-site set exactly.
Shipping the relaxation costs nothing that cannot be undone in a `WHERE` clause, and pinning the old
ingester would have cost the better table for the life of the release. **`RELEASES.md` records the
dependency explicitly** — that v0.1 carries a reading of QuantProject's text, not their ruling — so
nobody discovers it by archaeology.

Your `n_psms` correction is carried there too: the site *set* reproduces exactly, but 25 rows carry a
higher `n_psms` under the new reading. Thank you for correcting "exactly" rather than letting us find
it — that is the second time in two messages you have corrected your own claim before we hit it, and
it is why we are comfortable taking your recommendation ahead of the condition.

**One correction of ours, in the same spirit.** Our `RELEASES.md` recorded v0.1 as schema 0.0.2 /

## 3 · DATAREPO-18 · take your default for the protein N-terminus

`position = 0`, `residue = 'N-term'`, giving `…:A0AVT1:N-term0:UNIMOD:1`. It matches
`DEF-OCC-CELL`'s convention, which is what makes the two tables join, and the C-terminus mirroring it
as `length + 1` is right for the same reason.

Two things to flag rather than decide for you:

- **We have not asked QuantProject** whether they key a protein-terminal modification differently. You
  are right that a join key is expensive to change later, so we are asking them in the same message
  that carries the ambiguity-level question. If they disagree we will come back before R7 is populated.
- **`DEF-OCC-PSMS` excludes peptide-terminal modifications but counts protein-terminal ones** only when
  the peptide sits at that terminus (QuantProject 016, v3). So the thirteen `N-acetylmethionine on M`
  sites you found are exactly the population that should be representable, and your finding that
  `ptm_site_rows` skips them entirely is the right thing to have caught.
