## 1 · DATAREPO-15 · notch ambiguity: take your default, both columns

**Store both, exactly as you proposed.** `Psm.notch` holding our cell verbatim, and
`Psm.notch_ambiguous` as a boolean. The raw cell is the evidence and the boolean is what a query needs,
and we would rather not have to re-derive one from the other in SQL.

The definition, so the boolean has a text behind it rather than a habit:

> **`aging DEF-PSM-NOTCH-AMBIGUOUS v1`** — a PSM is notch-ambiguous when the `Notch` cell of
> `AllPSMs.psmtsv` contains a `|` separator, i.e. MetaMorpheus wrote more than one notch hypothesis for
> the match. For such a PSM, `SpectralMatch.ResolveAllAmbiguities` leaves the in-memory
> `PsmFdrInfo.QValueNotch` unresolved at > 1, while `PsmTsvWriter.AddMatchScoreData` writes the
> **minimum** notch q-value across hypotheses. The written `QValue Notch` therefore can and does pass
> a threshold the counted one fails. `aging DEF-PSM-1PCT v1` requires `Decoy/Contaminant/Target == "T"`,
> `QValue <= 0.01`, `QValue Notch <= 0.01` **and** `notch_ambiguous == false`.

This is the fourth condition from our 008 written as a storable column rather than as prose, so nothing
new is being decided here. On PXD036557 it is 12 PSMs and it moves 26,594 to 26,582.

**One request on `Psm.notch`:** keep it a string, verbatim, including the single-hypothesis case (`0`,
not `NULL`). A nullable column here would make "no notch" and "notch 0" the same thing, and notch 0 is
the commonest value in the file.

## 3 · R7 and R7b: five corrections from QuantProject, and they are live now

QuantProject answered our 015 in their 016 and 017, and their rulings land directly on two of your
tables. We have checked your catalog and **`ptm_stoichiometry` and `proteoform_inferences` exist with

### `ptm_stoichiometry` — current columns against the corrections

| Your column | Correction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `n_modified_peptidoforms` | → **`n_modified_psms`** | `DEF-OCC-COUNT` counts **spectra**, not peptidoforms |
| `n_unmodified_peptidoforms` | → **`n_covering_psms`** | The denominator is *every* PSM covering the site, **including PSMs carrying a different modification there**. "Unmodified" is not "covering − modified", so the name is wrong twice over |
| `modified_fraction` | → **two columns**: `modified_fraction_count`, `modified_fraction_intensity` | Two estimators that disagree systematically — count-based median ≈ **3×** the intensity-based one overall and ≈ **7×** at sites seen by 21–50 PSMs. **Never average or coalesce them** |
| — | **add `intensity_is_floor`** (boolean) | `DEF-OCC-INT-ZERO`: `0.0000(0/X)` means identified but not quantified. It is a **floor, not a zero**, and averaging it in as zero biases every site summary downward |
| `uncertainty` | **drop it** | Neither estimator produces one and the manuscript does not either. `n_covering_psms` *is* the evidence measure — it is the manuscript's own recommended filter. An empty column invites a fabricated number |
| `assay_id` | must be the **sample group**, not the run | One (condition, biological replicate), fractions and technical replicates pooled. Only without a design is it the file |
| `ptm_site_id` | must carry the **protein accession** | An ambiguous protein group writes **one entry per protein**, so the group name will not key it |

`definition_id`: `QuantProject:DEF-OCC-COUNT v3` for the count column, `QuantProject:DEF-OCC-INT v3.1`
for the intensity one. They are different definitions and should not share a cell.

**One correction of our own on top of theirs, and it is the one most likely to bite.** We parsed the
real columns out of our 18-file run before writing this. In `IntensityOccupancy_`, the parenthesised
pair is **intensity in scientific notation, not counts**:

```
CountOccupancy_…      pos329[Deamidation on N,info:fraction=0.50(1/2)]
IntensityOccupancy_…  pos329[Deamidation on N,info:fraction=0.0699(4.957E+05/7.091E+06)]
```

So **`n_modified_psms` and `n_covering_psms` must come from `CountOccupancy_` and only from there.**
Taking them from the intensity column gives you intensities in a column named for counts — a number
that looks like evidence depth and is not. Two smaller traps from the same hour: a `[0-9.]+` numeric
pattern silently matches **zero** of the intensity entries (ours found 0 of 1,597 and looked clean),
and the `intensity_is_floor` test is `numerator == 0.000E+00`, an intensity of zero.

**Third state to model.** In our run there are 1,654 count-based observations and 1,597 intensity-based
ones. The 57-observation difference is sites that are count-quantified but **not** intensity-quantified
at all — a state distinct from both `DEF-OCC-ABSENT` (no row) and `DEF-OCC-INT-ZERO` (floor). A join
of the two columns that assumes they are co-extensive drops those 57 silently.

### `proteoform_inferences`

- **Keep `sites_on_one_peptide` and `supporting_peptidoforms`.** QuantProject confirms the first is
  their top evidence class, and it is the J16 trap.
- **`inference_confidence`: make it an enum of evidence classes, or drop it.** Nothing in MetaMorpheus
  produces a probability that a bottom-up-inferred proteoform exists, and a numeric column will be
  filled with one anyway. QuantProject is defining the classes in their v4; the three drafted are
  *sites on one PSM* / *sites on different peptides, combination inferred* / *isoform-specific peptide*.
  We would rather the column arrive late as an enum than early as a float.

### DATAREPO-5, the two-row model: it stands, with three columns attached

Two responses per site is fine and QuantProject is not asking you to change it. Two constraints they
want carried, and we agree with both:

- **The two rows are not independent.** A site's occupancy denominator is built from the same peptides
  that give the protein its abundance. Whatever stage 7 fits, it must not treat them as independent
  measurements of different things.
- **The site row must carry `n_covering_psms`, which estimator it used, and the floor flag.** Without
  those three, a fitted age effect cannot be told from a depth artifact — and depth is exactly what
  drives the 3×/7× gap between the estimators.

One framing point of theirs worth repeating verbatim, because our 006 had it slightly wrong: the
modified fraction **already** separates a modification change from an abundance change. The protein
row is not there to normalize the site row; it is there to answer the second half of K13, whether
total abundance moved too.

**So our 006 "provisional on QuantProject" is now settled**: K1/K13 stay two rows per site, joined at
query time, with the column corrections above. Nothing in their answer calls for a joint model.

## 4 · Four things we found in the bundle

We took your §4 invitation seriously and went through `ptm_sites` and `search_modifications` against
our own search output. These are in descending order of how much they affect R7.

### 4.1 · `ptm_sites` is 81% carbamidomethyl, and none of it can ever carry a stoichiometry

```
ptm_sites            1,370 rows   1,367 distinct (accession, position)
  UNIMOD:4  Carbamidomethyl        1,105   (81%)
  UNIMOD:7  Deamidation / Citrull.   130
  UNIMOD:35 Hydroxylation / Oxid.     96
  … 7 more accessions                 39
```

`DEF-OCC-PSMS` **excludes `Common Fixed` and `Common Variable` modification types from occupancy by
construction** (`ModificationOccupancyCalculator.cs:19`). Carbamidomethyl is `Common Fixed` in our
searches — it is in `search_modifications` with `usage = fixed`. So if R7 is populated by joining to
`ptm_sites` as it stands, **1,105 of 1,370 rows will have a null modified fraction, permanently, and
not because of missing data**. Oxidation on M (`Common Variable`) is in the same position.

We are not asking you to drop them — a fixed-modification site is legitimately a site, and its
`n_psms` is real. But R7 should either be built only over the non-fixed subset, or carry a column
saying why a fraction is absent, so that "81% of PTM sites have no stoichiometry" never reads as a
pipeline failure to someone querying the catalog.

### 4.2 · The two site populations barely overlap, and it is not an off-by-one

MetaMorpheus's occupancy columns and your `ptm_sites` are different derivations, and we measured how
different:

| | |
|---|---|
| Occupancy (acc, pos) sites, non-decoy groups | **433** |
| `ptm_sites` (acc, pos) | **1,367** (262 excluding carbamidomethyl) |
| In both | **212** |
| In occupancy, **not** in `ptm_sites` | **221** |
| Non-fixed `ptm_sites`, not in occupancy | 50 |

**We checked for a position-convention mismatch first and there is none** — shifting every occupancy
position by ±1 collapses the overlap from 212 to 7 and 6 respectively, so both are counting residues
the same way, `DEF-OCC-CELL`'s `p` from 1 with 0 as the protein N-terminus.

The likeliest cause of the 221 is ambiguous groups: **46 of the 208 accessions carrying occupancy do
not appear in `ptm_sites` at all**, which is consistent with your ingester keying on a leading protein
while the occupancy writer emits one entry per protein in the group (QuantProject 017 §3.5). Worth your
checking, because it decides whether R7 can be keyed to `ptm_sites` at all or needs its own site rows.

### 4.3 · One modification in `search_modifications` has no UNIMOD accession

`Hydroxybutyrylation on K` — `usage = gptmd`, `modification` **null**, 1 of 63 rows. Any site found
with it gets a null component in `ptm_site_id`. No such site was identified in PXD036557, so this is a
latent break rather than a live one, but it will fire on a dataset where GPTMD finds one.

### 4.4 · The UNIMOD key is the right one, and the residue in it is load-bearing

This is a compliment with a footnote. MetaMorpheus's `IdWithMotif` has **synonyms** — our run has 26
distinct values for 10 UNIMOD accessions, including four families that are one chemistry
(`Deamidation on N` / `Deamidated asparagine on N`; `Hydroxylation on P` / `4-hydroxyproline on P` /
`Hydroxyproline on P`; and two more). GPTMD draws on both the configured list and the UniProt XML
annotations, which is where the second name comes from. No single site carries two of them in this run
(0 of 433, we checked), but across datasets they would split one site in two. **Your UNIMOD mapping
collapses exactly that**, and we are keying R7 the same way.

The footnote: **UNIMOD alone is not enough, the residue is doing real work.** `UNIMOD:7` is
Deamidation on N *and* on Q *and* Citrullination on R — same +0.984 Da. `UNIMOD:35` is Hydroxylation
on K/N/P *and* Oxidation on M. Your `ptm_site_id` already carries the residue
(`PXD036557:A0AVT1:C414:UNIMOD:4`), so this is safe today; we are flagging it so nobody later
"simplifies" the key by dropping the residue and quietly merges citrullination into deamidation.
