## 2 · DATAREPO-26 · **No C-terminal hole today — because nothing C-terminal has ever been searched**

You said you had no way to see it and we had the corpus. Measured three ways.

**(a) Nothing in the corpus is written as C-terminal.** ProForma marks a C-terminal modification as
`PEPTIDE-[mod]`. Across all three datasets, **0 peptidoforms use that notation**. All 1,696 that end
in a modification carry it on the last *residue* (`...DAVTYTEHAK[UNIMOD:35]`). So the writer is not
emitting C-terminal placements that we are failing to store — consistent with your reading that the
two are written identically.

**(b) No stored site carries a C-terminal chemistry.** Resolving each `ptm_sites.modification_name`
against the searching install's `PP` lines: of the **23,176 rows (65.1 %) whose exact `IdWithMotif`
resolves**, every one is `Anywhere.` — **0 C-terminal, 0 N-terminal**. (The unresolved 12,439 are §3.)

**(c) And here is the answer that matters: the searches never declared one.** Over the 63
modifications in `search_modifications`, by `PP` rule:

```
Anywhere.              23        C-terminal of any kind    0
Peptide N-terminal.     3        N-terminal.               2
unresolved             35
```

**Zero C-terminal chemistries were ever offered to the engine.** The five N-terminal ones are exactly
where S39 came from.

So: **the hole is latent, not absent.** There is nothing to recover today, and the day a search
declares a C-terminal chemistry — amidation is the obvious one, and it is a real biological C-terminal
modification — the same defect appears. And it will be **worse than S39 was**, because S39 at least
announced itself: a protein-N-terminal placement is written `[mod]-PEPTIDE` and we could count 3,085
of them. A C-terminal placement is written identically to a last-residue one, so there will be **no
signal at all**. You were right to hold rather than guess.

**Our recommendation:** do not implement C-terminal classification now. Instead, make it impossible to
miss later — when an ingest sees a declared modification whose `PP` is C-terminal, raise a `finding`
saying the classification is not implemented. That converts a silent future hole into a loud one, on
the day it first matters, and costs nothing until then.

## 3 · `PP` is the right discriminator — through `IdWithMotif`, never the accession, and not by parsing files

Two traps, both of which we fell into before getting an answer, so they are offered as measurements
rather than advice.

**Do not resolve `PP` through the UNIMOD accession.** One accession maps to many named entries with
*different* position rules — `UNIMOD:34` (Methylation) alone spans `Anywhere.`, `N-terminal.` and
`C-terminal.` across ~20 entries. Our first probe keyed on the accession and reported **247
C-terminal peptidoforms**. The true number is **0**. `peptidoforms` stores accessions, so **the
peptidoform table cannot answer this question at all**; `ptm_sites.modification_name` can, because
`IdWithMotif` names exactly one entry. That is a second argument for option (1), arriving from a
direction neither of us expected.

**And do not build the lookup by parsing the resource files — which is your G26, and we just proved
it on ourselves.** The two families spell `IdWithMotif` differently: MetaMorpheus's `Mods/*.txt` puts
the motif *inside* the `ID` line (`ID   Carbamidomethyl on C`), while UniProt's `ptmlist.txt` keeps
`ID` bare with the motif on `TG`. Our attempts, in order:

| approach | result |
|---|---|
| match on the stripped base name | 100 % "resolved" — and `Acetylation on K` silently got `Acetylation on X`'s **protein-N-terminal** rule |
| always append `TG` | 8.1 % resolved; `Carbamidomethyl on C` became `Carbamidomethyl on C on C` |
| handle both spellings | 65.1 % resolved, and correct as far as it goes |

Three attempts, two of them confidently wrong, on a file format we have on disk. **This is your 021
§5 finding reproduced against us**, and it is why the PP lookup should come from mzLib's loader
through pyMzLib rather than from anyone's parser — the same `REQ-PYMZ` route you took for G26. Our
remaining 34.9 % are names whose entries live in sources this probe does not read, `unimod.xml` chief
among them, which is precisely the source your 021 §5 said your own registry never reads.

## 4 · §6 · `search_modifications_placed` derives from the **peptidoforms**, not from `ptm_sites`

You asked rather than guessed, and the answer is the one your question implies.

**The view exists to be trusted about absence, so it must draw from the table that loses nothing.**
`ptm_sites` is per *resolved protein position*, so it drops every placement without one — and we now
know exactly how much that is: the **210** occupancy sites at `pos0`, the **264** with no determinate
position, and before 0.8.0 the **1,367** protein-N-terminal sites. A "what was actually placed" view
built on `ptm_sites` would have reported that N-terminal acetylation was **never placed** in any of
our three datasets, while 3,085 peptidoforms carried it. That is S39 reproduced in a new table, which
is the thing you were trying not to do.

**One limitation to state in the view's description rather than discover later:** a ProForma string
carries an accession or a mass, never an `IdWithMotif`. So the placed view is at **accession-or-mass
grain**, and it cannot name the chemistry the way `ptm_sites` can. For a J8 mass-silent check that is
the right grain anyway — the question is about masses. But it means `_declared` and `_placed` are not
directly comparable row-for-row, and the description should say so.
