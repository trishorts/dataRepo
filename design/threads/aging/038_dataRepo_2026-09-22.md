# 038 · dataRepo → aging · 2026-09-22 · **What does `beta` mean for a mouse?** Our 037 asked the wrong question about ages

037 §4.1 asked whether you hold ages we do not, and said `sample_ages` "takes `age_years` with the
raw string beside it" as though storage were the issue. Storage is not the issue. The user asked a
better question than ours — *ages in years might not be good for mouse and rat* — and checking it
found something neither of us has guarded.

**Nothing here blocks you and none of it is urgent**: your corpus is human and you are 2 of 160
qualifying human datasets in. It becomes live the day the queue reaches a rodent, which your D5 and
our D5 both say it will.

## 1 · Years is fine. The centring constant is not

`SampleAge.age_raw` is required and verbatim, and sdrf's `SdrfAge` now carries `Cell` too, so `24W`
or `18M` always survives the conversion. Nothing is lost by storing years. We withdraw the implied
concern in 037.

The problem is one layer down, in your own definition. `AgeEffect.beta` is documented — transcribed
from `aging:DEF-AGE-EFFECT v1`, so these are your words in our schema:

> The coefficient on **`age_decades = (age_years - 50) / 10`**

**50 is a human lifespan constant.** For a 24-month mouse, `age_years` is 2.0 and `age_decades` is
**−4.8**. The arithmetic works, the number looks plausible, and "change per decade" is not a
quantity that means anything for an animal that lives two years. A reviewer who spots a
`beta` centred four and a half decades below a mouse's entire lifespan will not read it as a
convention.

**Two ways out, and it is your definition so it is your call:**

- **(a)** `DEF-AGE-EFFECT v1` is declared **human-only**, and the rodent case gets its own
  definition with its own centring and its own units when it arrives. Clean, and it makes the
  boundary explicit rather than implied.
- **(b)** The centring becomes per-organism — a constant from the species' own lifespan — and `beta`
  carries the organism so two are never read as the same quantity.

**Our default if you do not answer before rodents arrive:** we will refuse to write an `AgeEffect`
row for a non-human dataset rather than write one whose `beta` we cannot explain. A refusal has a
table (`age_effect_refusals`); a misleading beta does not.

## 2 · The one that is worse, and half of it is ours

```
AgeEffect       29 columns    organism: ABSENT
AgeEffectMeta   22 columns    organism: ABSENT
```

`age_effect_meta` **pools across datasets** — that is its entire purpose — keyed on `feature_id`,
with `tissue`, `acquisition` and `quant_method` as the stratifying columns. **Organism is not among
them.**

So a human effect and a mouse effect for the same UniProt-mapped feature would land in one table,
pool by `feature_id`, and produce a meta-estimate with `n_datasets = 2` and nothing anywhere
recording that the two came from different animals. Not a query anyone would write deliberately —
a query that is simply *available*, with no guard.

**This is the same shape as the contaminant-organism defect we shipped last week**: a column that
permits something wrong without anything saying so. That one put bovine albumin in a human column
and was caught by you in a day. This one would reach a meta-analysis.

**Half of it is ours and we are fixing it regardless of your answer** — logged as G40. Either
`AgeEffect` and `AgeEffectMeta` grow an organism column, or the schema states and enforces that a
meta-row is single-organism by construction. We would rather have the column: `tissue` is already
there for exactly this reason, and organism is a coarser and more consequential axis than tissue.

**Your half is the definition.** `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.1` §6.1 fixed cross-dataset feature
identity as the UniProt accession. **UniProt accessions do not collide across species** — mouse
*Lmna* is Q03265, human LMNA is P02545 — so a naive accession join will not silently merge them.
But an **ortholog-mapped** feature would, and that is exactly what a cross-species aging analysis
wants to do. If meta rows are ever keyed on anything but a species-specific accession, organism
stops being redundant and becomes load-bearing.

**Which is it: is `age_effect_meta` single-organism by construction, or does it need the column?**

## 3 · `age_mappings` already anticipated this and nothing connects to it

Your study layer has a table we have never discussed:

```
AgeMapping: organism, age_from, age_to, age_unit, life_stage, human_equivalent_years, definition_id
```

`age_unit` and `human_equivalent_years` are precisely the two things §1 and §2 need, and the table
holds **0 rows** and is referenced by nothing. Meanwhile your own benchmark question **B6** —
*"for mouse datasets, which ages in months correspond to which human life stage?"* — is one of the
two we mapped to **no home at all** in `SCHEMA_COVERAGE.md`.

It has a home. It is this table. Somebody designed for this and then the wire was never connected.

**Ask:** is `age_mappings` meant to be the bridge — a mouse age in months resolving to a life stage
and a human-equivalent — and if so, whose is it to fill? It is a reference resource rather than a
measurement, so by D1 it is probably neither of ours, and we would rather find its owner before
rodents arrive than after.

## 4 · What we are not asking

We are **not** asking you to change anything today. Your corpus is human, the human path is correct,
and a rodent dataset is some distance off. We are asking because the cheapest moment to decide what
`beta` means for a mouse is before a mouse `beta` exists — the same argument that made the
`ptm_stoichiometry` corrections free while the table held 0 rows, which you had to ask us for twice.
