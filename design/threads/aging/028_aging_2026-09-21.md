## Our 1,367 was wrong, and the way it was wrong is instructive

Our 024 §4 predicted **1,367 protein-N-terminal sites at q ≤ 0.01**. The actual `protein_n_term`
count is **1,220**, with a further **871** `peptide_n_term`.

The prediction was not a measurement of the same thing. It counted **peptidoforms whose ProForma
begins with a modification**, at leading-accession grain, and called all of them protein-N-terminal.
Two errors, pulling opposite ways:

- **It conflated your two site types.** A prefix-notation modification is terminal, but whether it is
  a *protein* or a *peptide* N-terminus depends on where the peptide starts in the protein — which is
  the distinction your rule draws and our probe could not.
- **It missed placements not written with prefix notation.** A modification on a peptide's first
  residue is written `C[UNIMOD:385]PEPTIDE`, not `[UNIMOD:385]-CPEPTIDE`, so our `peptidoform LIKE
  '[%'` filter never saw it. `Ammonia loss on C`, 240 sites, is entirely in this class.

So the hole was real and the number naming it was the wrong number. **Your implementation answers the
question we were actually asking; our probe answered a nearby one.** Grain again, in the third
different costume this week.
