## 2 · §4.2 · We checked, and it is our ambiguity filter, not your protein groups

You asked us to check whether the 221 are ambiguous groups, because that decides whether R7 can key
to `ptm_sites`. We reproduced your measurement (we get 425/208/217 where you get 433/212/221 — a
parsing difference in how we split the occupancy cells, not a disagreement) and then asked, for every
missing site, **which accepted PSMs actually cover that residue**:

| Of the 217 occupancy sites missing from `ptm_sites` | |
|---|---|
| Covered by an accepted **level-1** PSM | **0** |
| Covered **only** by accepted PSMs at **ambiguity level ≥ 2** | **181** (83%) |
| Covered by **no accepted PSM at all** | **36** |

Counted by accession rather than by site, the **75** accessions carrying a missing site break down
as: 4 never appear in any accepted PSM, 20 appear only above level 1, and **51 do appear at level 1**
— so for two thirds of them it is the site we dropped, not the protein. Separately, of the 46
accessions absent from `ptm_sites` altogether, only **20** are in a multi-protein group at all. The
ambiguous-group story, even where it applies, is not what is driving this.

**The actual cause is a rule of ours, stated in the code and not in any thread.** `ptm_site_rows`
emits a site only from a PSM at **ambiguity level 1**, on the reasoning that anything else is "a site
the evidence does not place". Against the 18-file run that is 22,730 accepted PSMs kept and 4,027
dropped, and level 2D alone is 3,651 of them. Your occupancy calculator evidently uses a wider
population — the 36 covered by no accepted PSM say it is wider than "target at 1%" as well.

**So the answer to your question is: R7 can key to `ptm_sites`, but only if `ptm_sites` stops being a
level-1-only table.** As it stands, keying R7 to it would silently drop 83% of the sites you can
actually compute an occupancy for. This is ours to fix, not yours.

### What we need from you to fix it (DATAREPO-17)

**Which PSM population does `DEF-OCC-PSMS` count over?** Specifically: what q-value cut, and which
ambiguity levels. Our default if you do not answer: **emit a `ptm_site` from any accepted PSM whose
modification position is determinate, whatever its ambiguity level, and add a column recording the
best (lowest) ambiguity level that placed it** so a caller can filter to level 1 and reproduce
today's table exactly. That is additive and loses nothing, but it is a guess about your population
and it will not explain the 36.

A worked example of one of the 36 would settle it faster than a specification.

### A latent bug we found while checking, now fixed

Not live, but it would have become live the moment we relaxed that filter, which is what makes it
worth telling you about. A peptide shared between proteins starts at a **different residue in each**,
and MetaMorpheus writes one span per protein, `|`-separated and aligned with the accession column.
Our site derivation took the **first** span and applied it to every accession — correct for the
leading protein, wrong for all the others.

PXD036557 has **3,154** accepted PSMs whose accessions carry differing spans. **None is at ambiguity
level 1**, which is the only reason this has never produced a wrong position. The fix pairs each
accession with its own span, and we verified it leaves `ptm_sites` **byte-identical** on your data —
1,370 rows in, 1,370 identical rows out. **Your v0.1 pin is unaffected and stays correct.**

We mention it because the two findings compound: relaxing the ambiguity filter without this pairing
would have put wrong residue positions into the repository, on exactly the multi-protein peptides
that the relaxation is meant to recover.
