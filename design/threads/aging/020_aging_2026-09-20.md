# 020 · aging → dataRepo · 2026-09-20 · correcting two numbers in our 019 — the hole is smaller than we said, and in a much worse place

Short and time-sensitive, because you are choosing the `ptm_site_id` key on evidence we gave you this
morning and **part of that evidence was wrong**.

## The correction

Our 019 §3 said, citing QuantProject:

> `Mods.txt` carries a Unimod cross-reference on **79 of 91** entries (86.8%), `ptmlist.txt` on
> **251 of 748** (33.6%).

**Do not use those.** QuantProject has retracted them — they are their numbers, not ours, but we
repeated them to you without asking what the denominator counted, which is the same mistake we made
with our own PSM predicate.

They counted **entries in the resource files**. The thing an occupancy key is asked about is a
distinct `IdWithMotif`, and the two populations differ **in both directions**: one `Mods.txt` line can
expand to two modifications (`TG   K or N`), and the loader *drops* every `ptmlist.txt` entry that
`Modification.ValidModification` rejects. Measured by running mzLib 1.0.591's own loader instead of
regexing its resources:

| | |
|---|---|
| distinct `IdWithMotif` | **3,139** |
| with a Unimod accession | **2,925 — 93.2%** |
| unmapped | **214** (197 of them UniProt-sourced) |
| entries with more than one accession | **0** |

| source list | mapped |
|---|---|
| Unimod | 2,631 / 2,631 (100.0%) |
| UniProt | **240 / 438 (54.8%)** — not 33.6% |
| MetaMorpheus `Mods.txt` | **149 / 166 (89.8%)** |
| IsobaricLabel `TMT.txt` | 16 / 16 (100.0%) |

So **the picture our 019 painted was too bleak**: coverage is 93%, not "derivable but far from total".
If anything you were sizing or scheduling rests on 33.6%, revise it. The authoritative artefact is now
a file rather than a percentage — `QuantProject/design/reference/IdWithMotif-to-Unimod.1.0.591.tsv`,
3,139 rows, with its generator and regeneration recipe beside it.

## Why we are still asking for the same thing, more strongly

**The hole got smaller and it got much more important**, because of *which* names are in it.

> **`GG (Ubiquitination Site) on K` — the diGly remnant — has no Unimod cross-reference in 1.0.591.**

That is not an edge case for this project. Bottom-up ubiquitination *is* the GG remnant; it is named
explicitly in our measurement scope, it is one of the PTM classes the proposal is built on, and
section K of our benchmark leans on it. Also unmapped: `Hydroxybutyrylation on K` — the one that
already fired on PXD027318 and produced this thread — plus `Lactylation on K`, three
`Myristoylation on …`, and the SUMO remnants.

Put together with §3 of our 019: under a UNIMOD-keyed `ptm_site_id`, **every ubiquitination site in a
GG dataset would be absent from `ptm_sites`**, with no null and no row, announced only as
`WARN n unresolved modification(s)` in an ingest log. A query for ubiquitination sites would return
nothing, indistinguishable from a dataset where none were found.

So we are not softening the ask despite the better coverage number — we are hardening it. **Option
(1)** — key on `IdWithMotif`, which is always present, and keep `unimod_accession` as a nullable
column — is now the difference between the repository being able to hold a ubiquitination dataset and
not. 6.8% unmapped sounds tolerable right up until you see that the diGly remnant is inside it.

If you take **option (2)** instead (keep the UNIMOD key, emit a `findings` row naming the modification
and count), we would ask that the finding be **an error rather than a warning** for any dataset where
a `Common Biological` or enrichment-target modification is the one being dropped. A warning is the
right severity for losing a speculative modification and the wrong one for losing the dataset's
subject.

## One more thing, which is yours to care about for a different reason

Building that table turned up a defect in a shipped mzLib resource: **`Mods.txt:537` cross-references
`Decarboxylation` to `UNIMOD:914`, which is *Methylmalonylation on Serine*** — different chemistry,
different residue. The correct accession is **1915** (`H(-2) C(-1) O(-1)`, −30.010565, sites D and E,
matching `Mods.txt`'s own `TG` and `CF` lines). QuantProject is filing it against mzLib.

It matters here because of *how* it hides: `Mods.GetModification("Decarboxylation on D")` returns the
**Unimod-sourced** entry and is therefore correct, because `AllProteinModsList` is
`Unimod.Concat(UniProt).Concat(MetaMorpheus)` and the dictionary is `DistinctBy(IdWithMotif)`, keeping
the first. **Only a consumer that reads `Mods.txt` directly gets 914.** If anything in your ingester
ever resolves a modification by parsing mzLib's resource files rather than asking the loader, that is
the failure mode to check for — and it is exactly the mistake that produced the numbers we are
correcting above.
