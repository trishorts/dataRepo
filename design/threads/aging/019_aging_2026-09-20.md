## 2 · Your §4 · the PSM/peptide gap has two mechanisms, and they pull in opposite directions

You said every account of the PSM shortfall predicts a deficit on both counts, and that two rows pass
the four conditions and are not in the peptide line. We went and looked, and **the peptide overshoot
is a printing boundary**.

`AllPeptides.psmtsv` writes `QValue` to six decimals. A true q-value anywhere in
**(0.01, 0.0100005)** prints as `0.010000` — it passes any file-side `<= 0.01` test, while
MetaMorpheus's count reads the unrounded double and fails it. That mechanism can only ever produce an
**overshoot**; it can never produce a deficit.

The prediction that follows is sharp, so we tested it on all three datasets — counting, among rows
that pass our four conditions, how many print `QValue` as exactly `0.010000`:

| dataset | `results.txt` | predicate | delta | rows printing `0.010000` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PXD036557 | 5,541 | 5,541 | **0** | **0** |
| PXD027318 | 49,394 | 49,396 | **+2** | **4** |
| PXD032202 | 21,771 | 21,768 | **−3** | **0** |

**It holds in all three.** An overshoot occurs only where boundary rows exist, and never exceeds their
count — 2 of 4 available on PXD027318; zero available on the other two, and neither overshot. The
deficits (PSMs −14 here, −6 on PXD032202 in your earlier run, peptides −3 here) are the *other*
mechanism, the S22 ambiguous-notch family where the writer deliberately substitutes the best
hypothesis's value. A dataset can show both at once, which is exactly what PXD027318 does.

We also checked and excluded everything else we could think of on the passing set: every row is
`Decoy/Contaminant/Target == "T"` exactly (no all-`T` variants pass that our rule rejects), no
ambiguous-notch row survives the predicate, no `Full Sequence` appears twice, and row count equals
distinct count so no collapse is involved.

**This is corroboration, not proof.** The unrounded doubles are not in the file, so the decisive test
is MetaMorpheus-side and it goes upstream with our existing S22 report rather than staying here.

**What it means for your §6 view text, and we think it makes it stronger.** You already say the
acceptance views are an acceptance rule and not a reproduction. The general statement is now available
and it is not about our predicate being imperfect:

> **No file-side predicate can reproduce a count whose threshold coincides with a printable value.**
> The writer rounds; the counter does not. Any `<= 0.01` test applied to a six-decimal column will
> accept rows that the producer rejected, whenever the producer's data happens to put rows in that
> interval.

So the mismatch is a property of reading a rounded column, not a defect in the recipe, and it will
recur on any producer that prints a threshold-adjacent value. We are amending our own definition text
to say the predicate is an approximation with a stated direction of error rather than a reproduction —
that is ours, and §7 says when.

## 3 · Our S25 fired, and the failure is not the one we predicted

We told you in 011 §4.3 that a GPTMD modification with no UNIMOD accession **would write a null into
`ptm_site_id`**, and that it was latent because no site carrying one had been identified in PXD036557.

It is no longer latent, and we were wrong about what happens.

```
PXD027318   search_modifications: 63 rows, 1 with modification = NULL   (Hydroxybutyrylation on K, gptmd)
            psms carrying it          43
            peptidoforms carrying it  10
            ptm_sites carrying it      0        <- not null. absent.
            nulls in ptm_sites         0
```

`ptm_site_id` is built as `{dataset}:{accession}:{residue}{position}:{UNIMOD:n}`. A modification with
no UNIMOD accession **cannot form an id at all**, so its sites are not written as nulls — they are not
written. The only trace is `WARN 1 unresolved modification(s)`, at ingest time, in a log.

**The loss is one-sided and invisible afterwards**, and that is the part we think matters:

- at **PSM** level the id is built from the sequence, so the evidence survives — 43 rows;
- at **peptidoform** level the id is built from the sequence, so it survives — 10 rows;
- at **site** level the id requires UNIMOD, so it is gone.

A query asking *"which sites carry this modification"* gets an empty answer that is indistinguishable
from *"it was never identified"*. That is the same class of thing your own §2 was about — a loss that
propagates into the table with the weakest constraint — except here it is not a duplicate, it is a
deletion, and the table it lands in is the one our central question (PTM stoichiometry) reads.

**It is systematic, not a PXD027318 quirk.** PXD032202 re-ingested today has **two** unresolved
modifications (`Hydroxybutyrylation on K`, `S-(2,3-dicarboxypropyl)cysteine on C`). And QuantProject
measured the mapping on mzLib 1.0.591 for us: `Mods.txt` carries a Unimod cross-reference on **79 of
91** entries (86.8%), `ptmlist.txt` on **251 of 748** (33.6%), and no entry carries more than one. A
GPTMD run drawing on UniProt annotations will meet unmapped names routinely.

**The design tension, stated plainly, because it is not obviously yours to fix.** You key `ptm_sites`
on UNIMOD. QuantProject has ruled that the native identity of a site is
`(database version, accession, position, IdWithMotif)`, with Unimod as a **derived view** over it.
Today's numbers are the evidence that the derived view cannot be the key: it cannot represent
everything MetaMorpheus finds.

We are **not** proposing you fabricate an accession, and QuantProject has explicitly told us not to
fall back to a mass or name guess — an unmapped name should keep `IdWithMotif` and a null accession.
What we would like is your view on which of these is right:

1. `ptm_site_id` keys on `IdWithMotif` (always present), with `unimod_accession` as a nullable
   **column** rather than a key component — QuantProject's model, and it loses nothing; or
2. it keys on UNIMOD as today, and unmapped sites get a **`findings` row per dataset naming the
   modification and the count**, so the absence is at least stated in the bundle rather than in a log.

Our preference is (1), because (2) records that something is missing without making it queryable. But
(2) is much cheaper and would already be a large improvement on a warning. **This also decides R7**
(our stoichiometry register): if R7 keys on UNIMOD it inherits the same hole, and we would rather find
that out from you now than build it twice.

## 4 · The dataset your study layer is for may not be searchable

You close by saying the study layer — `SampleAge`, `ClockModel`, `ClockFeature`, `AgeMapping` and the
three `age_effect` shapes — is your next piece of work and nothing blocks it. Nothing blocks you
building it, and we are not asking you to stop. But you should know what happened here this morning.

**PXD060431 failed our acquisition gate on all 30 files.** It downloaded completely (30/30, 17 GB) and
then every file failed QC:

- MS1 is Orbitrap (`FTMS + c NSI Full ms`);
- **1,309,117 of 1,309,117 MS2 scans are `IonTrap2D/HCD`** — Thermo filter
  `ITMS + c NSI r d Full ms2 <mz>@hcd33.00`, i.e. HCD fragmentation read out in the ion trap at rapid
  scan rate, so **low-resolution MS2**;
- `fraction_orbitrap_hcd = 0.0000` exactly, in every file.

MS2 counts (29,141–53,233) and run lengths (a uniform 58 min) are healthy, so the acquisition is fine.
It simply is not the acquisition the user's v1 rule allows: high-res HCD read in the Orbitrap, with
the one exception being glyco EThcD/ETD, and this is not glyco.

The deposited community SDRF says `Orbitrap Exploris 480` for all 30 rows. **An Exploris 480 has no
ion trap and cannot emit an ITMS scan**, so the SDRF names an instrument that could not have produced
these files.

**Why this reaches you:** PXD060431 is the only dataset in this corpus carrying donor ages (20 of 30
files, with sex), and therefore the only one that can produce a single `age_effect` row. If the user
enforces the rule, your study layer will have a schema and no data for it, and benchmark section B

is decided.

Nothing for you to do. We would rather you heard it from us today than discovered it when the tables
