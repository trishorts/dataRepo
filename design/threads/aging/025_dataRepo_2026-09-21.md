## 1 · S39 is closed, and your condition is met

The `continue` is gone. Terminal placements are written, keyed on **the residue they sit on**, with
`site_type` joining `ptm_site_id` **only when it is not `residue`** — which is the whole mechanism
that keeps your released ids where they are.

Verified by re-ingesting across the change and diffing the site table:

```
sites                       33 -> 36
ids LOST                     0        <- your condition
carried-over rows that
  changed ANY value          0
ids gained                   3        all three terminal
```

The three:

```
PXD999999:P63261:E2:N-acetylglutamate on E@protein_n_term
PXD999999:Q9UL25:A2:N-acetylalanine on A@protein_n_term
PXD999999:O75396:C52:Ammonia loss on C@peptide_n_term
```

**And the first two are why `start == 1` would have been wrong.** Both sit at **residue 2**, because
co-translational N-terminal acetylation follows initiator-methionine excision — your own §4 named
acetylation as the dominant chemistry, so a naive rule would have mislabelled the single most
abundant terminal mark in the proteome as `peptide_n_term`. The derivation we implemented is:

> peptide starts at residue 1, **or** starts at residue 2 with a previous residue of `M`
> → `protein_n_term`; any other terminal placement → `peptide_n_term`.

The `Ammonia loss` row is the counter-case that stops the rule collapsing into "everything terminal
is a protein terminus": span `[52 to 81]`, an internal position, so `peptide_n_term`.

`residue` is now nullable as you specified, and for a terminal site holds the residue the
modification actually sits on rather than the string `N-term`.

## 2 · Your other two implementable asks, in your shapes

**§7 `permitted_responses` — an allow-list, and it is in `CONTENT_FIELDS`.** Your argument carried
both halves. It is a real column on `Dataset`, not a flag string, so a query can honour it without
parsing prose; it is positive, so a response type invented later is excluded rather than silently
permitted; and changing it **re-identifies the bundle**, because two bundles over the same rows —
one usable for site localization and one not — are not the same object. A test asserts the
re-identification, sitting directly beside the one asserting that rewording a `reason` does *not*.
The column is empty for every dataset until you fill it.

**§2a the definition register — your correction, not our offer.** We had offered the check against
the core `definitions` table; you were right that their definitions have no business in a search
bundle. So a delivery declares its own:

```yaml
definitions:
  - aging:DEF-AGE-EFFECT
  - aging:DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META
```

and a `definition_id` not among them refuses the write. One deliberate softness: **a delivery that
declares no register is not checked.** A producer who has not adopted it should not be silently held
to a stricter contract than the one they agreed to; declaring a single definition opts fully in.
`examples/study_delivery/` now ships the register, so copying it opts you in from the first delivery.

**§2b `delivery` being advisory — already true, and already proved.** It was never in the content
hash: `STUDY_NON_CONTENT_FIELDS` classifies it as prose alongside `notes` and `instance`, and
`test_the_producers_prose_does_not_move_the_id` asserts that two deliveries differing only in that
label land on the same bundle id. Your 021 §4 lesson was applied here before you asked.

## 3 · What we did not do

**`search_modifications` is not yet split (§6).** We agree with the rename and with
`_declared` / `_placed` rather than leaving either holding the unqualified name. What we will not
guess is **which table the placed view derives from.** `ptm_sites` is per protein position; your own
measurement came from peptidoform ProForma, and those two differ for any placement with no resolved
protein position. Since a "what was actually placed" view exists precisely to be trusted about
absence, picking the narrower source silently would reproduce S39's shape in a new table. **Which
source do you want?** Tracked as our G31.

**C-terminal placements are not classified, and this is a limitation rather than a decision.**
`...K[mod]` is written identically whether the modification is on the peptide's last residue or on
its C-terminus. The only thing that could separate them is the mod file's `PP` line, which
`modlist` does not parse. Guessing would move existing ids on no evidence, so those rows stay
`residue` — which is what they have always effectively been. **DATAREPO-26: is there a C-terminal
hole of the same shape as S39, and if so is `PP` the right way to find it?** We would rather ask than
measure it wrong; you have the corpus.

**G26 stays sequenced where you put it (§5).** Your point that option (1) already defused it — a
registry miss now writes `modification = NULL` and the row survives — is the part we had not fully
internalised. We will take the `REQ-PYMZ` route and ask pyMzLib for the loader's own call rather
than consume a file, and when the fallback does fire it will raise a `finding` naming the mzLib
version and the path taken, as you asked. Not in this release.

## 4 · Your §1 answer lands on our side too

**Accession, with the join through membership in `protein_accessions`.** We had marked both halves
ASK and you confirmed one and corrected the other, with numbers: 2,608 pooled against 2,497, and the
111 lost being the paralogue families rather than a tail. The `protein_group_id`-is-the-whole-member-
list observation is a better argument than the one we gave for why the id cannot cross a dataset —
we had said "it has a prefix", and you showed it is a function of group composition, which is a
function of which peptides were observed.

Practically: `age_effect_meta.feature_id` is an accession, and the join to dataset-level evidence
goes through membership, never through `feature_id = protein_group_id`. We have **not** yet added
the referential check for it — it is buildable now and is our G32 — because we would rather land it
with the membership-join view than as a bare constraint.

## 5 · One sentence of your ruling contradicts your own example

Worth flagging rather than quietly resolving. Your §4 says:

> `residue` stays nullable and stays **out** of the key.

but the example in the same paragraph is `PXD036557:P16401:M1:Acetylation on X@protein_n_term`, in
which `M` is the residue and it is plainly *in* the key. The two cannot both hold, and the tie is
broken by your own condition: **removing the residue from the key would move every existing id**,
which is the one thing the ruling forbids.

So we implemented the example. `residue` is nullable as a *column*, and the key still carries it.
Tell us if you meant the other reading, because the two differ for every row in the repository and
this is the last cheap moment to say so.
