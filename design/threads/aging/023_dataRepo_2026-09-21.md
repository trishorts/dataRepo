## 1 · We built ahead of your answer, and here is exactly how reversible it is

The thing that made this safe to build is the same thing that made it worth building: **a study
bundle is a separate object**. It is not a new field in a search bundle and it is not a new stage in
`ingest`. So the blast radius of us being wrong about your hand-over is one function.

What we have committed to, and what we have not:

| | Settled by us | Reversible if you disagree |
|---|---|---|
| A study layer's rows arrive **separately** from a search, and loading them never re-ingests | yes — and this is the part we think is not really a choice | the alternative forces a re-ingest for every re-fit |
| A delivery is **content-addressed** like a bundle, so a re-fit lands beside the old one | yes | — |
| Loading a delivery is **opt-in** at build time and moves the catalog id | yes | — |
| **The file format you hand over** — one file per table, `.tsv`/`.csv`/`.parquet`, columns named as the schema names them | **no — this is a guess** | `study.read_table_file` changes; the bundle and catalog contracts do not |

So: if stage 7 writes something else, tell us and we change a reader. If you want the rows to arrive
some entirely different way, we have lost one module and no data. We are tracking the unsettled part
as our G30 rather than pretending 20(a) is closed.

**What we still need from you is unchanged**: 20(c) is the expensive one, and it is in §3.

## 2 · What a delivery looks like

A `study.yaml` beside your output files:

```yaml
study_manifest_version: 1
layer: aging
instance: ncems-aging
delivery: stage7-2026-10-02
store: F:/aging_data/repo/store     # the same store the search bundles are in
tables:
  age_effects: age_effects.tsv
  age_effect_refusals: refusals.tsv
  age_effect_meta: meta.parquet
  sample_ages: sample_ages.tsv
```

Then `datarepo study study.yaml`. The bundle lands under `<store>/_study/aging/<bundle-id>/`.

Three things worth knowing before you write the first one:

- **Columns are named as the schema names them.** `schema/study/aging.yaml` is the list, and it is
  your `DEF-AGE-EFFECT v1` transcribed column-for-column.
- **The delimiter comes from the file extension and is never sniffed.** A guess turns one column of
  `A;B` into two on somebody else's machine.
- **In a text file, a list column splits on `;`.** That is what carries
  `age_effect_meta.dataset_ids` — because C3 and D1 need *which* datasets pooled, not how many.
- **Deliver only the tables you have.** An omitted table stays empty, and empty is an answer: your
  own benchmark distinguishes `NO_TABLE` from `EMPTY_TABLE`.

A worked, tested delivery is committed at `examples/study_delivery/`. Copy the folder.

## 3 · What we refuse — and the two things we deliberately do not check

**Your definition's rules are enforced at write time, not documented beside the column.** These are
write errors, not warnings:

- A `beta` with no `se` is not an age effect. Same for `age_span_years`, `covariates`,
  `normalization`, `method`, `method_version`.
- A refused fit has **nowhere** to write a null `beta` — `age_effect_refusals` has no such column.
  Your §5 ("no row, not a row with a null") is enforced by there being no row to write.
- A column name the layer does not have stops the write and names it. A typo'd header is not
  silently dropped.
- Two rows for one fit stop the write. Which of two answers is right is yours to say, not ours.
  (The keys were declared in 0.6.0 while the tables were still empty, exactly so this check existed
  the moment something filled them.)

At **build** time we also refuse an `age_effects.dataset_id` the catalog does not hold, rather than
dropping the row. A silently smaller answer to section D is the failure mode we keep writing to you
about.

**And now the two we do not check, because checking them would answer your questions for you:**

- **`feature_id` resolves against nothing.** This is **DATAREPO-20(c)**, still open, and it is the
  one that gets expensive. A `protein_group_id` in this repository is scoped to its dataset, so it
  cannot be a cross-dataset key, and §6 does not say what replaces it. A foreign key written now
  would freeze our guess with the authority of a constraint. **This is the answer we most want.**
- **`definition_id` is not resolved against the core `definitions` table.** Whether a study layer's
  own definitions land in a search bundle is not settled, and refusing every delivery over it would
  enforce a contract you never agreed to. Say the word and it becomes a check.

## 4 · This release costs you nothing

**`bundle.INGESTER_VERSION` stays 0.5.0 and not one search bundle id moves.** Nothing in the ingest
path reads, parses, derives or writes anything differently — writing a study bundle does not even
*open* a search bundle, and a test asserts that byte for byte.

`CATALOG_VERSION` went 2 → 3 and `catalog_id` now hashes the loaded delivery ids, which is the
correct half of the same rule: a catalog holding age effects and one without them answer 46 of your
benchmark questions differently, so they must not share an id.

By the rule we gave you in 022 §4 — **a datarepo release re-ingests your store only when
`INGESTER_VERSION` or the schema version moves** — 0.7.0 does not. 0.5.0 did; 0.6.0 and 0.7.0 do
not.
