## 1 - What 0.19.0 changes

- **The contaminant flag on `proteins` is now per accession.**
  - **What was wrong:** MetaMorpheus writes `Decoy/Contaminant/Target` once per peptide-protein
    match, not de-duplicated, and writes `Accession` de-duplicated (`PsmTsvWriter.cs:229, 258`,
    MetaMorpheus `6e152da70`). So the two cells cannot be zipped. We gave every accession on a row
    the row's worst letter.
  - **Effect on the corpus:** 494 rows move from contaminant to target (278 human, 127 mouse, 89 rat)
    and 67 go the other way.
  - **Now:** `proteins.is_contaminant` agrees with MetaMorpheus's own single-protein group label in
    all 65,012 cases. Before, it disagreed 76 times.
- **An upstream provenance file is read only if it is still the file the search recorded.** Your
  search provenance's `upstream[]` carries each file's sha256, and we never checked it. Now a file that
  no longer matches is left out, with a finding naming both sha256s. On your corpus this is the
  `db_prepare` record of **all 24** (§2).

We verified by re-ingesting all 24 to a scratch store: the catalog passes 399 checks and 427 tests
pass.

## 3 - How MetaMorpheus decides T or C, from its source

Your 061 says the search decides `T` or `C` by which database hit first. The source says otherwise
(MetaMorpheus `6e152da70`, mzLib `8931f219`):

- **Which databases are contaminant is decided per FILE.** A database whose path contains
  "contaminant" or "CRAP" is a contaminant database (`CMD/Program.cs:261, 368-374`).
- **Duplicates are resolved before the search.** After loading, `SanitizeBioPolymerDatabase` groups
  every entry by accession and applies `TCAmbiguity` (`DatabaseLoadingEngine.cs:190-278`).
  - The default, and **the value in your search tomls**, is `RemoveContaminant`.
  - Under it, the contaminant copy of any accession that is also in the target database is dropped,
    with a warning in the log.
- **So in your results, an accession in both databases is always a target, `T`,** with the target
  database's organism. Nothing depends on which one "hits first".

**The count you asked for.** These are primary accessions of `MetaMorpheusContaminants.xml` (264
entries) that are also in the searched proteome:

| proteome | shared with the contaminant panel |
|---|---:|
| human (UP000005640, 2026_09_18) | **116** (e.g. P02768 albumin, O43790, O76009, O76011, O76013, O76014, P00441) |
| mouse (UP000000589) | **26** |
| rat (UP000002494) | **0** |

**What that means for DEF-QC-9,** as we read it:
- Under `RemoveContaminant`, none of those 116 human proteins is ever a contaminant in your results.
  Human keratins and human serum albumin are therefore counted as target.
- Your suspicion is right, that a contamination metric reading MetaMorpheus's `C` understates them.
  The reason is `TCAmbiguity`, not which database hits first.
- `RemoveTarget` and `RenameProtein` are MetaMorpheus's other two settings, and choosing between them
  is your science.

We can send the 116 as a file, or the one-line method to regenerate them.
