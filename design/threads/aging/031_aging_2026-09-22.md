your schema forced and our text had left open:

**§6.1 — cross-dataset feature identity.** `feature_id` at meta grain for `feature_type =
protein_group` is a **bare UniProt accession**, and the mapping from a dataset-grain row goes through
`protein_groups.protein_accessions` **membership**, never through the id string and never through a
leading accession. The measurement is in your 024 copy already: of 2,608 accessions in ≥ 2 datasets,
131 lead in some and not others and 50 never lead, so a leader-keyed join pools 2,497 against 2,608
and the 111 lost are the paralogue families. `feature_type` is in the key. For `ptm_site` the identity
is `(accession, residue, position, chemistry)` off your `ptm_sites_by_chemistry` view — not the
UNIMOD column, which would put 38 site keys behind a NULL.

**§6.2 — the shared beta, which is the part we had not written down anywhere.** A group has one beta;
under membership pooling that beta is assigned to each member accession. So feature counts inflate by
**1.02–1.05×**, and paralogues sharing a group are **perfectly correlated by construction** — one
number written *n* times. A meta-analysis treating CALM1/2/3 as three independent observations of a
compartment trend is counting one measurement three times.

**The new column that falls out of it: `n_source_groups`, beside `n_datasets`** — the number of
distinct protein groups a pooled estimate was built from. When it is less than `n_datasets`, the row
is drawing on shared betas. That is ours to fill, not yours to check; we are telling you because it
is a column and you asked to be told about columns before they set.

**§6.3 — the `stratum` grammar.** Stays a string, as you defaulted. But the grammar is fixed even
though the vocabulary is not: `all`, or lowercase `key=value` with `key` from a named list that may
grow. A producer-side rule — nothing changes on your side. It exists so that `sex=F` and `sex=female`
cannot both appear and pool neither.
