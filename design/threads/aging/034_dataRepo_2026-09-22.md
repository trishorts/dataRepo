## 2 · Contaminant species (and a new column)

`Protein.organism` is now **the taxon of the database the entry came from**, and NULL for
contaminant-panel and decoy entries. New **`Protein.organism_name`** carries MetaMorpheus's own
species string verbatim — it was there the whole time and we were discarding it:

```
organism_name among your PXD032202 contaminant rows:
  683  Bos taurus          323  Sus scrofa        286  Mus musculus
   25  Ovis aries           23  Escherichia coli (strain K12)
   15  Equus caballus       10  Gallus gallus      9  Hathewaya histolytica ...
```

**No name-to-taxon mapping happens in dataRepo** (D1). Turning `Bos taurus` into `NCBITaxon:9913`
is a reference resource somebody owns and we do not; a caller that needs the CURIE joins one.
Logged as **G36**, and it is a candidate `REQ-PYMZ` if the FASTA headers carry `OX=` — worth
knowing whether pyMzLib can hand us the taxon rather than the name, since you will hit this
properly the moment the queue reaches mouse.

One thing worth flagging for your rodent datasets specifically: with the old behaviour a human
contaminant panel in a mouse dataset would have been labelled `NCBITaxon:10090` — species evidence
**inverted**, not merely flattened. That is now impossible.

`Protein.organism` was `required: true` and that is precisely what produced the defect. **A
required column with no true value gets a false one.** It is optional now, which is the general
lesson rather than the fix.

## 3 · The MCP round, since it came out of the same run

Everything in §4 of 033 is done, and the one that matters is the first:

- **`datarepo_sql` now returns `tables_touched`, and `empty_tables` when any is empty.** Six of the
  seven near-misses were that single gap. Every "this table is empty, do not answer from it" guard
  lived in `describe` and `search` — the two tools an agent may skip — and was missing from the one
  `describe`'s own "what to do next" block points at.
- **The answer to D12's fourth-tool question is NO**, and the argument is the benchmark agent's
  rather than ours: *a fourth tool would have to be chosen, and an envelope field cannot be
  skipped.* We are taking that as settled unless your own run disagrees.
- Provenance is now **validated against `catalog_bundles`** instead of read off a column's name. A
  bundle id existing in no catalog anywhere was being returned as the provenance of 8,055 real
  rows. That was our D13 claim failing in the one direction we had not looked.
- Per-column non-null counts everywhere, because table-level emptiness was only half of it: your
  `samples` holds 57 rows with `organism_part`, `cell_type`, `disease`, `condition` and `cell_line`
  NULL on every one, so `search("plasma")` came back `rows: 57, hits: 0` — which reads as a
  considered no.
- The derived tables (`protein_index`, the `_1pct` views) are documented at last. They were the
  tables `search` answers from and they carried **no column descriptions at all**, which is how
  `n_datasets_1pct` got read as "identified at 1% FDR" when it means peptide-**or**-protein-level
  acceptance — 879 of 9,130 pairs it counts are absent from `protein_groups_1pct`.

**Relevant to you directly:** `quant_values.value` now says in its description that it holds
intensities **and** spectral counts in one column, separable only by `definition_id`. An agent took
a median across the bare column and reported a spurious million-fold LMNA difference between two of
your datasets. That is U8's grain rule broken *inside* a single column, which we had not considered
as a failure mode.

measurement that counts is yours: 032 §3 still stands — point it at your catalog when the batch
leaves you a gap, and send back the answers that came out **confidently wrong**, not a percentage.

entirely in the derived layer — the part we had documented least.
