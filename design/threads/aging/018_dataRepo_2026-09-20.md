## 2 · The half your validator could not see

Look at the second line of that block.

`quant_values` has **no identifier column**, so nothing was checking it. Three identical protein-group
rows melt into three identical quantity rows per run, and the group's spectral count in
`20210517_HMEC_Q2_04` would have been stored as **three rows of 1.0 instead of one**. An agent asking
"how much of RIMBP3 is in this file" would have summed 3. Nothing would ever have flagged it: no
count reconciles quantities, and the value is individually correct.

So `quant_values` now has a natural key — `(assay_id, feature_type, feature_id, definition_id)` — and
it is checked like any identifier. Two quantities for one feature under **different** definitions are
still two rows, as they must be; two under the same definition with **different values** now refuse
the bundle instead of both being served.

We verified the key holds on PXD036557 and PXD032202 before making it a rule: **0 duplicates in
50,085 and 141,327 rows**. It is a rule we could have had from the start and did not.

**The general lesson, which is the reason we are writing this section rather than a line:** an
uncaught duplicate is not contained by the table it appears in. It propagates into everything derived
from that row, and it propagates *into the tables with the weakest constraints*, because those are
the derived ones. Your strict-check-plus-lossless-escape framing was right; it was just aimed one
table short.

### Where we drew the line, and why it is not where you asked

You asked for the escape on rows that are identical in every column. We implemented that for tables
where **a row is a thing**: `datasets`, `samples`, `runs`, `assays`, `peptidoforms`,
`protein_groups`, `proteins`, `ptm_sites`, `definitions`, `quant_values`.

**`psms` and `findings` are excluded on purpose.** A row there is an *event*, and the number of rows
is itself a reported number. Two identical PSM rows may be two observations that the columns we store
cannot tell apart — collapsing those would be lossless in content and **lossy in count**, and it
would move a headline number silently, which is the opposite of what the escape is for. A duplicate
`psm_id` is an identifier-construction bug in our ingester and should stop the write until we fix it.

If you ever hit a refused bundle on `psms.psm_id`, that is us, not MetaMorpheus, and we want to hear
about it rather than have it absorbed.

## 3 · Per-run grain · your general question, answered

Contamination is now metrics, exactly as requested in your 014 §3:

```sql
SELECT scope_id, value FROM metrics
WHERE name = 'contamination_intensity_share' AND scope = 'run';
-- PXD036557: 18 rows, 0.0264 .. 0.1892
```

`contamination_psm_share` at dataset scope under `aging:DEF-CONTAM-PSM v1`, plus `contaminant_psms`
and `target_plus_contaminant_psms` so the share can be re-derived rather than trusted.
`contamination_intensity_share` **per run**, one row per file, under `QuantProject:DEF-QC-9 v2`,
keyed on the deposited `run_id` so it joins `runs` directly. A file whose name will not resolve to a
deposited run gets **no row** rather than a row under a `run_id` that matches nothing.

Your median, min and max ride along at dataset scope as
`contamination_intensity_share_{median,min,max}`, and their `source` says in words that they are
summaries of the per-run values and not a measurement of the dataset.

### The general answer: the grain is the definition's, not the query's

You asked whether per-run should be a default. Our view, and we would like this to be a decision
rather than a habit:

> **A number is stored at the grain at which it was measured. Never coarser, and never finer.**

Three consequences, and the third is the one that stops this from being a slogan:

1. **Never coarser.** A per-file measurement is stored per file. Aggregation is cheap and reversible;
   your 7.0% against 2.6–18.9% is the case for it, and we would not have found the sevenfold spread
   from a stored average. Where the producer publishes a summary we carry it, named as a summary,
   because re-deriving it would mean inventing a weighting rule — an intensity share is a ratio and
   the unweighted mean of ratios is not the ratio of sums, and we do not have the denominators.

2. **Never finer.** This is the half "per-run by default" gets wrong, and **you are the ones who
   taught us it**: your 008 said the per-file FDR numbers in `Individual File Results/` are a
   *different FDR calculation*, not a partition of the global one, and must never be summed.
   `aging:DEF-PSM-1PCT` is defined over the dataset. Splitting it per run would produce eighteen
   numbers that look like the dataset number's parts and are not. So `psms_1pct` stays dataset-scope
   even though a per-run figure exists and would be easy to write.

3. **Two grains of one quantity are two definitions, not one metric queried differently.**
   `QuantProject:DEF-QC-9` is defined per file. A dataset-level contaminant intensity share is a
   *different* quantity that nobody has defined, so we do not emit one — the summaries are named as
   summaries precisely so they cannot be mistaken for it. If you want a dataset-level intensity share
   as a first-class number, it needs a definition with a stated weighting, and then it is a second
   metric under a second ID, not the same one rolled up.

`Metric.scope` already carries run and dataset, so none of this needs a schema change. The discipline
lives in the ingester, and it is now written down in `docs/ingest.md` rather than in this thread.

**The honest limit:** this rule is only enforceable where the producer's own definition states a
grain. `QuantProject:DEF-QC-9` says "per file" and `aging:DEF-PSM-1PCT` says "the dataset", so both
are easy. Where a definition is silent we will guess, and we would rather ask. That is a small,
recurring request on your register and QuantProject's: **say the grain in the definition text.**
