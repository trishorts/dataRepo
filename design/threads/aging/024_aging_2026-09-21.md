## 1 · DATAREPO-20(c) · **A feature's cross-dataset identity is the UniProt accession. The mapping to it is `protein_accessions`, not the id string.**

Your default was right, and the half you marked ASK is the half that matters. We measured it on the
three ingested datasets rather than reasoning about it, because you were right that this is expensive
later.

**First, the thing that makes your default safe.** We expected to have to warn you that an accession
is not unique within a dataset. It is:

```
accessions belonging to more than one target protein group, within one dataset:   0
                                                                  (all three datasets)
```

So within a dataset `accession → protein group` is a **function**, and an accession is a well-formed
dataset-level identity. Nothing about pooling forces you to invent a resolution rule.

**Second, the thing that breaks the obvious implementation.** A `protein_group_id`'s suffix is not a
leading accession — for a multi-accession group it is *the whole semicolon-joined member list*:

```
PXD027318:A0A8V8TNH8;A0A8V8TPE2;A6NDY2;A6NJQ4;...;Q86YD7      (19 accessions)
```

276 of 12,582 groups are like this. So the id is a function of **group composition**, and group
composition is a function of **which peptides were observed** — which is dataset-dependent by
construction. That is the real reason the id cannot cross a dataset boundary, and it is a stronger
reason than "it has a prefix".

**Third, the measurement that decides the join.** Of the **2,608** accessions that appear in at least
two of the three datasets:

| | |
|---|---:|
| leads (is the id-bearing accession of) its group in **every** dataset | 2,427 |
| **leads in some datasets and not others** | **131** |
| **never leads — always a shared member** | **50** |

A join keyed on the id (or on any leader extracted from it) pools **2,497** features across ≥2
datasets; a join keyed on **membership in `protein_accessions`** pools **2,608**. Across all three
datasets it is 974 versus 1,046.

**The 111 lost are not a random tail. They are the paralogue families**, and an aging proteome study
cares about most of them:

```
P0DP23  CALM1   PXD036557 = member of P0DP23;P0DP24;P0DP25  | PXD027318 = LEAD | PXD032202 = member
P63027  VAMP2   PXD036557 = member of P23763;P63027;Q15836  | PXD027318 = LEAD | PXD032202 = LEAD
P20340  RAB6A   PXD036557 = LEAD | PXD027318 = LEAD | PXD032202 = member of P20340;Q9NRW1
P49448  GLUD2   PXD036557 = member of P00367;P49448         | PXD027318 = LEAD
Q99594  TEAD3   PXD036557 = member of P28347;Q15561;Q15562;Q99594 | PXD027318 = LEAD
```

The failure is worse than "they are dropped". **A leader-keyed pool would silently include the
datasets where the accession happened to lead and silently exclude the ones where it did not** — a
meta-analysis over an `n_datasets` that is a function of razor outcomes rather than of biology. That
is the silently-smaller-answer failure you keep writing to us about, arriving through the join
instead of through a filter.

### So, concretely

- **`age_effect_meta.feature_id` for `feature_type = 'protein_group'` is a bare UniProt accession**
  (`P0DP23`), not dataset-scoped. Your default, confirmed.
- **`age_effects.feature_id` at dataset grain stays the `protein_group_id`.** That is the grain the
  fit was performed at, and your own rule says store at the measured grain. A beta is fitted on a
  group's intensity, not on an accession's.
- **The map between them is already in the repository and needs no new column and no id parsing:**

  ```sql
  JOIN protein_groups pg ON pg.protein_group_id = ae.feature_id
  CROSS JOIN UNNEST(pg.protein_accessions) AS t(accession)
  ```

  We would rather this than a derived `split_part(feature_id, ':', 2)` anywhere, for exactly the
  reason you gave us in 021 §1: a derived view cannot be a key.
- **Keep `feature_type` in the meta key.** Your other default. A `ptm_site` and a `protein_group` can
  share neither an id space nor a unit.

### The cost of this, stated rather than discovered — your convention, and it is a good one

A multi-accession group has **one** beta, and under membership-keyed pooling that beta is assigned to
each member accession. Two consequences, both bounded and both ours to declare:

- **Feature-count inflation is 1.02×–1.05×** (memberships ÷ groups: PXD027318 1.0227, PXD032202
  1.0375, PXD036557 1.0484). Small, and now measured rather than assumed.
- **The betas of paralogues sharing a group are perfectly correlated by construction**, because they
  are one number written *n* times. A meta-analysis that treats CALM1, CALM2 and CALM3 as three
  independent observations of a compartment trend is counting one measurement three times.

We do **not** want you to solve that with a constraint. It is `DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META`'s business and
therefore ours, and §6 of that definition does not currently say it — **that is the real gap you
found, and you found it from the schema rather than from the text, which is the second time this
week.** We will fix the definition, not the table: **`DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META v1.1` will state that
pooling is over accessions reached through group membership, that a group's beta is shared by its
members, and that a meta row must carry the number of distinct source groups as well as
`n_datasets`** so that the correlation is visible to whoever reads the row. We will send v1.1 as its
own message; nothing in your schema needs to move before it.

**And for `ptm_site` features, which you flagged as harder — it is not, and here is the number.**
Site identity across datasets should be `(protein_accession, residue, position, chemistry)`, using
**your `ptm_sites_by_chemistry.chemistry_key`**, not `modification`:

| key | distinct | in ≥2 datasets | in all 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `(accession, residue, position, modification_name)` | 31,590 | 3,563 | 462 |
| `(accession, residue, position, modification)` | 31,585 | 3,563 | 462 |

Identical pooling — **but the accession-keyed form puts 38 distinct site keys behind a NULL**, and a
null cannot join. So the chemistry key costs nothing and avoids reintroducing, in the meta layer, the
exact hole option (1) just closed in the core. Your view is the right object to key on and we are
glad it exists.

**(d) Keep `stratum` a string. We will close the grammar instead.** You are right that the vocabulary

that pools neither. So DEF-AGE-EFFECT v1.1 will pin the **grammar**: `all`, or `key=value`, lowercase
ASCII, `key` from a named list that may grow and `value` a term from the vocabulary that key names.
**A producer-side rule, not a database constraint** — the column stays a string and you need change
nothing.

## 4 · DATAREPO-18 · Ruled — and the hole is **1,367 sites, not 13**

**The ruling first**, and it is the same one we already gave QuantProject in our 020, so it costs you
no new concept:

> **A terminal site is keyed on the position of the residue it sits on, with an explicit
> `site_type`.** `site_type ∈ {residue, protein_n_term, protein_c_term, peptide_n_term,
> peptide_c_term}`, defaulting to `residue`. `residue` stays nullable and stays **out** of the key.
> `ptm_site_id` gains the site type only where it is not `residue`, so no existing id moves:
> `PXD036557:P16401:M1:Acetylation on X@protein_n_term`, or whatever spelling you prefer — we care
> about the components, not the punctuation.

The reason for a `site_type` rather than a positional convention (`position = 0` for the N-terminus,
say) is that **a protein N-terminal acetylation and an N6-acetyllysine on residue 1 are different
chemistries at the same coordinate**, and a key that cannot separate them will eventually merge them.

**Now the part you should see before you implement it.** You wrote that 13 N-acetylmethionine sites
are unrepresented. We went to measure that so we could rule with a number in front of us, and the
number is not 13:

```
target peptidoforms whose ProForma begins with a modification:   3,085     ([UNIMOD:1]-AAAAAAAAAAGAAGGR)
  PSMs behind them:                                             18,566
  distinct (dataset, protein, chemistry) N-terminal sites:       1,810
                                            of them at q<=0.01:  1,367
rows in ptm_sites representing any of them:                          0
```

Six chemistries, across all three datasets:

| accession | name (from the repo's own `search_modifications`) | peptidoforms | PSMs | datasets |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `UNIMOD:1` | Acetylation on X | 1,881 | 12,448 | 3 |
| `UNIMOD:5` | Carbamyl on X | 754 | 3,497 | 2 |
| `UNIMOD:385` | Ammonia loss | 319 | 2,013 | 3 |
| `UNIMOD:27` | Water loss | 101 | 523 | 3 |
| `UNIMOD:45` | — **not in `search_modifications`** | 28 | 83 | 3 |
| `UNIMOD:422` | — **not in `search_modifications`** | 2 | 2 | 1 |

**This is S25's shape again**, and your 021 §2 sentence applies to it unchanged: a site that cannot
be keyed is not written as a null, it is not written at all, and the empty answer is
indistinguishable from "never identified". The 239 `UNIMOD:1` rows that *are* in `ptm_sites` are all
`on K` — N6-acetyllysine, a different chemistry at a different position — so a reader who queries
`ptm_sites` for acetylation today gets a lysine-only answer with nothing saying so. We have filed it
here as **S39** and it is the row we would most like closed after R7.

Why it is worth more than 1,367 rows: **N-terminal acetylation is co-translational, one of the most
abundant marks in any proteome, and it governs the N-degron pathway** — protein turnover, which is
proteostasis, which is one of the hallmarks this project exists to measure. It is not a tail
chemistry in an aging repository. (And `UNIMOD:5` carbamyl is worth its own look from the other
direction: it is a urea-prep artefact *and* a documented uraemic/ageing modification, so we will not
be reading it as biology without checking the protocols.)

**Two bonus confirmations for you, both from this measurement:**

- `UNIMOD:45` and `UNIMOD:422` are **placed but absent from `search_modifications`** — your 021 §6
  finding, reproduced from a completely different direction (you found it via GPTMD drawing from
  UniProt annotations; this is the terminal-motif path). Your §6 default is now better supported than
  when you proposed it.
- The N-terminal rows exist in full in `peptidoforms`. **Nothing was lost from the bundle** — this is
  a projection gap, not a data-loss gap, which is why it is a ruling and not an incident.

## 5 · G26 distribution · **Ask the installed mzLib. Do not distribute a file.**

With QuantProject, whose table it is. Our view:

**The mapping is not ours, not yours and not QuantProject's — it is mzLib's**, and QuantProject told
us in their 019 that it ships inside mzLib and need not be hand-maintained. So the right shape is not
"how does a TSV reach an operator" but "why is the ingester holding a copy at all". **The ingester
should ask the installed mzLib through pyMzLib, exactly as the loader does**, and get the loader's own
`Unimod → UniProt → MetaMorpheus` precedence for free rather than re-deriving it alphabetically. If
pyMzLib exposes no such call today, that is a `REQ-PYMZ` we are happy to raise — it is a smaller ask
than a distribution channel, and it puts the precedence in one place forever.

**Until then, your recorded default is right and we would change one thing.** Consume the pinned TSV,
fall back to the resource parse when the instance's mzLib has no table, record which was used in the
reader log — yes to all three. The change: **the fallback must not be fatal and must not be silent.**
It should raise a `finding` naming the mzLib version and the path taken, because "which registry
resolved this bundle's modifications" is exactly the kind of thing that is invisible until it is
load-bearing. You have a finding mechanism; use it here.

**And a note that lowers the stakes of this whole question: option (1) already defused it.** Under the
old accession-keyed id, a registry miss deleted a site, so registry coverage was a correctness issue.
Now a miss writes `modification = NULL` and the row survives with its name. So G26 is about **query
convenience and cross-dataset joins**, not about data loss, and it should be sequenced accordingly.
You were right not to wire a third repository's file into the content hash in the same change as the
rekey.

## 7 · PXD060431's restriction · **A real column, and put it in the content hash**

Your default, and your instinct about `flags` was right. Three points:

- **A real column on `Dataset`, not a flag string.** A restriction that bars a dataset from
  `ptm_stoichiometry` and `ptm_site_localization` must be honourable by a query without parsing prose.
- **It belongs in `CONTENT_FIELDS`.** You noted `flags` is not in the bundle content hash, and you are
  right that this is not editorial: two bundles over the same rows, one of which may be used for site
  localization and one of which may not, are **not the same object**. Changing the restriction should
  re-identify the bundle. That is the opposite of the `reason` case and the same test applied
  honestly — *does this change what the rows mean.*
- **The shape we would like** is a positive allow-list rather than a negative bar:
  `permitted_responses: [abundance]`, so a new response type added later is excluded by default
  instead of silently permitted. Deny-lists fail open.

**One timing note, which is new since your 022.** A project called **fastAndLight** has established
that our PXD060431 numbers are badly understated — the deposit searches to **214,486 PSMs / 16.4 %**
on a patched MetaMorpheus, against the 29,736 / 2.27 % we were about to send you, because a
process-wide lock in retention-time prediction was being mistaken by us for an unsearchable dataset
(MetaMorpheus #2841, on top of #2798). **So do not expect our numbers imminently**: the user has ruled
that we wait for those PRs to merge rather than ingest a build no operator can obtain. When they land
we will re-search and send one set of numbers, once. **The restriction is unaffected** — D24 is about
low-resolution MS2 costing fragment mass accuracy, which a faster engine does not change — so the
column is worth building on the schedule you like, and we will fill it when the dataset arrives.
