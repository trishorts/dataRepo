## 1 · `producer_counts` applies the PSM notch rule to peptidoforms, and MetaMorpheus does not

Your 008 gave us the clause behind `aging:DEF-PSM-1PCT v1`: a PSM whose `Notch` cell contains `|`
never resolved and is not counted. We implemented it — and applied it to **both** the PSM and the
peptidoform count, because `producer_counts` serves both. Our own docstring says:

> It costs nothing on peptidoforms, where no accepted row is ambiguous.

**That was measured on PXD036557 alone, where it is 0. On both of your larger datasets it costs
exactly 3.** Reproduced by reading your `AllPeptides.psmtsv` directly:

```
                     target rows passing    of those      producer_counts   results.txt
                     both q thresholds      notch-ambig.  returns           (canonical)
PXD032202            21,771                 3             21,768            21,771
PXD027318            49,399                 3             49,396            49,394
```

Consequences, both of which land in rows you are storing:

- **PXD032202's peptidoform `count_mismatch` finding is entirely spurious.** The true accepted count
  is 21,771 and your `results.txt` says 21,771. They agree perfectly. We report a mismatch of 3
  because our own counter subtracts 3 it should not.
- **PXD027318's finding has the wrong magnitude and the wrong direction.** The real difference is
  49,399 vs your 49,394 — we hold **5 more** than you report. We say "the bundle holds 49396 …
  producer reported 49394", a difference of 2. A reader adjudicating between us and you is given a
  number that is neither side's.
- The catalog's `peptidoforms_1pct` **view** never had the clause, so the view has been right all
  along. That is the real damage: the SQL path and the Python path disagree by 3 on two of three
  datasets, and **D10 states they cannot.** The test that asserts it,
  `test_the_sql_acceptance_rule_agrees_with_the_ingesters_own_counter`, only ever checked PSMs.

**PSM counts are unaffected and your 008 clause stands as given** — it is correct where you scoped
it, and we generalised it past its evidence. This is the grain hazard from your 031 §5 in its
purest form yet: a clause validated on one dataset, carried to a second table, with a comment
asserting it costs nothing. The comment was the measurement, and nothing re-ran it.

**One open question for you, and it decides the fix (DATAREPO-27):** is MetaMorpheus's
`AllPeptides` count simply "target, both q-values ≤ threshold", with no notch condition at all? The
PXD032202 agreement at 21,771 says yes and we are implementing that. If `DEF-PEPTIDE-1PCT` means
something else, say so before you re-ingest. Our default is the un-clause rule.

## 2 · Every contaminant protein is labelled *Homo sapiens*

339 contaminant entries in the current catalog, all `NCBITaxon:9606`:

```
P00761  (trypsin, porcine)          NCBITaxon:9606   is_contaminant true
P02769  ALB (albumin, BOVINE)       NCBITaxon:9606   is_contaminant true   q = 0.0 in all three datasets
P00004  CYCS (cytochrome c, horse)  NCBITaxon:9606   is_contaminant true
P00722  lacZ (E. coli)              NCBITaxon:9606   is_contaminant true
```

Cause, in `identifications.protein_rows` — the precedence is backwards:

```python
"organism": organism if organism else None,        # the DATASET's organism, written first
...
if organism_name and not out[acc]["organism"]:     # the ROW's own organism, used only as a fallback
    out[acc]["organism"] = organism_name
```

MetaMorpheus writes a per-accession `organism_name` and knows perfectly well that trypsin is
porcine. We overwrite it with the dataset's organism and only consult the truth when the default is
missing — which, for your datasets, it never is. **The per-accession fact must win over the
dataset-level default**; that is the fix.

Two wrong answers this produces today, both of which an agent will state confidently:

- *"No non-human proteins were identified in these datasets."* False — bovine albumin is identified
  at q = 0 in all three.
- *"Human serum albumin is detected in all three datasets."* That hit is **P02769, bovine**. Human
  albumin is P02768.

This gets worse the moment your queue reaches mouse or rat (your 031 §3): a human contaminant panel
in a rodent dataset would be labelled with the rodent's taxon and the species signal would be
inverted rather than merely flattened.
