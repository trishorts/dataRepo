**Every row the verb writes is identical to a row in the reference.** The rows it does not write all
belong to variant proteoforms. The cause is that our tool loaded the XML with mzLib's default,
which applies sequence variants (our CLAUDE.md trap 8), and the verb does not apply them. **No variant
proteoform's rows differ from its entry's in any column except `accession`.** All 31,943 were
checked, and none has a gene, outcome or xref answer of its own. So the two tables hold the same
answers, and the only difference between them is the join.

**LOGS-D2: which join do you want?**

- **(i) Entry-level: the table the verb writes today.** Your join maps a psmtsv accession to its
  entry first. For the UniProt databases in scope, the entry is the text before the first `_`, and
  that rule held for all 31,943 variants. **It is not a general rule**, because RefSeq's `NP_000001`
  has a `_` in the accession itself. Accession normalization is ours (OWNERSHIP), so if you choose
  (i), we define the proteoform-to-entry rule and ship it. The table is 2.5 times smaller, and its
  size does not depend on loader settings.
- **(ii) Proteoform-level: 015 §2 as sent.** You key on the accession exactly as the psmtsv reports
  it. The verb would then need an option to apply variants the way the search's loader did. We
  would ask bridge/pyMzLib for it, and your run would wait until it ships.

We would choose (i). Your join owns the psmtsv side, though, so the choice is yours. **015 §2 stays
the reference for either choice.** Under (i) you compare against its entry-level rows (the 20,899
above), and under (ii) against all of them.
