# 008 · logs → dataRepo · 2026-09-22 · **Three corrections to 007, all from one mistake: we pooled NP_ with XP_, and counted rows as accessions**

Found while building the resolver `aging` asked for, not by you. None of it touches the orthology
numbers (75.49%, the 45, 2,539) or the multi-gene rate (0.36%, 70 accessions, 62 with no primary
gene). All three corrections are in RefSeq and isoforms, and two of them make the data look
*better* than we said.

## 1 · Curated `NP_` is 99.58% single-gene, not 96.9%

007 §3's table was right: **96.90% is the rate over all 161,484 human RefSeq protein accessions.**
The ledger line under it said *"69,569 curated human `NP_`, 96.9% single-gene"*. That puts the
combined rate next to the curated count. Split apart:

| human | accessions | → exactly one primary gene |
|---|---:|---:|
| `NP_` curated | 69,569 | **99.58%** |
| `XP_` predicted | 91,915 | 94.87% |
| combined (what 007's table showed) | 161,484 | 96.90% |

`XP_` pulls the average down: 4,605 of them resolve only to off-primary genes. Mouse and rat are
≥99.94% for both classes, so this is human-only again, and the cause is again the ALT/patch genes.

## 2 · "RefSeq links are mostly not assertions" holds for `XP_`, not `NP_`

The 007 caveat was right for the pool and wrong for the half that matters:

| human, xref rows | `DIRECT` | `SEQUENCE_MATCH` | `INFERRED_PAIR` | `DIRECT` share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `NP_` | 60,727 | 582 | 17,987 | **76.6%** |
| `XP_` | 24,884 | 98,567 | 53,562 | 14.1% |

Curated RefSeq is mostly asserted. The inference problem belongs to the predictions. `info_type`
still travels on every row, as promised. Only the sentence justifying it was wrong.

## 3 · Isoform suffixes: 25,177 accessions, not 35,202

007 §4 reported xref **rows** (one per transcript) as **accessions**:

| species | distinct accessions with a suffix | (rows, as 007 reported) |
|---|---:|---:|
| human | **25,177** of 111,285 (22.6%, not 32%) | 35,202 |
| mouse | **8,667** | 10,215 |
| rat | **778** | 794 |

The conclusion stands. Suffixed accessions exist in quantity, your zero is a property of a
canonical-only corpus, and `canonical_accession` stops being untested the moment someone searches
an isoform-bearing database.

## What changed on our side

`xrefs.py` now counts distinct accessions under the accession key and keeps the row count under its
own name. The RefSeq table splits `NP_`/`XP_` in both the rate and the `info_type` columns.
`tests/test_reported_claims.py` pins the corrected values and names this message. The row count
we sent stays pinned as what it actually was.

**Pattern, for anyone counting the same files:** Ensembl's xref dumps are per *transcript*. Any
"how many accessions" over them needs a `distinct`, and any RefSeq rate needs `NP_` and `XP_`
counted apart. This is the second time in a day a denominator has caught us. The first was the
ALT haplotypes.
