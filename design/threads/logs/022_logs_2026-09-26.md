## 1 - Why rat resolves poorly, measured on `abf612c9…` (8,228 entries)

Read off the rat XML and our `search_db_rat_e116.tsv` today, not from memory:

| XML outcome | entries | what the XML links | Ensembl's xref recovers |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| `resolved` + `multi_gene` | 4,227 | an `ENSRNOG00000…` gene (the GRCr8 gene set) | n/a |
| `off_primary_only` | 950 | **only** `ENSRNOG00055…`, `…00060…` or `…00065…` ids, none of them in the GRCr8 gene set (727 link all three) | **521** |
| `not_in_source` | 3,051 | **no Ensembl gene id at all** | **204** |

- UniProt's rat XML links **four** Ensembl gene-id series: `ENSRNOG00000` (4,227 entries),
  `00060` (4,122), `00055` (4,119), `00065` (4,106). Only the first is the assembly our gene set is
  built from. The resolver is right to hold the other three to the gene set: they are not genes of
  the assembly the release describes.
- 521 + 204 = **725** entries that Ensembl's own xref resolves and the XML does not. They are rows
  with `source = ensembl_xref` and `ensembl_xref_agrees = true`, carrying the XML's `outcome`. That
  is why counting `outcome` understates the agrees view.
- **Entries with at least one `ensembl_xref_agrees = true` row: 4,908 of 8,228 (59.6%).** Under that
  view 3,320 (40.4%) have no gene. Human is 94.7% by the same count.

So rat is still the weakest species by a wide margin, and that comes from the reference data, not
the searches, as you said. But "about half" is the XML's view, and aging does not use it.

**What we did not establish:** why 3,051 reviewed rat entries carry no Ensembl link at all. That
is UniProt's cross-referencing for GRCr8, and we have not asked them.
