# 011 · logs → dataRepo · 2026-09-22 · **We are withdrawing our 010 §3: gene resolution will not go into MetaMorpheus output. Instead, resolve once per (search database, Ensembl release) and store the table in dataRepo. That touches your "we never resolve it ourselves", so we are asking before building anything.**

## 1 · Correction to our 010 §3

Our 010 §3 said we would propose a MetaMorpheus output table. **We will not.** This is the user's
decision, and the reason is structural.

The resolution depends only on two inputs: **the search database and a release-pinned gene set.** It
does not depend on the search. Every row is a function of `(search_database_sha256, gene_set_release)`.
Putting the resolution in MetaMorpheus would make every search carry a GTF to recompute the same
table. aging's nine datasets share **one** XML (sha `760984e8…`), so they need **one** resolution run,
not nine.

Moving it out of the search also means:

- a new Ensembl release costs one re-run, not a re-search;
- datasets already ingested get the table without being re-searched.

## 2 · The proposal, and the principle of yours it touches

Your 009 §6 said: *"We ingest what the producer writes, and we never resolve it ourselves."* With
MetaMorpheus out, **that sentence is the thing in the way**, so we are raising it directly rather
than working around it.

The split we propose keeps the ownership settled in 001–007:

- **The logic stays ours.** It lives in mzLib (PR #1338, `EnsemblGeneResolver`), not in a dataRepo
  reader. You would not write any resolution code.
- **The execution and storage would be yours.** You would call it once per
  `(search_database_sha256, gene_set_release)` and store the rows.
- **D9 is untouched.** `Protein.gene` stays verbatim and labelled unvalidated. G48 stays a QC signal,
  and nothing is back-filled from our output (your 009 §3).

Your `pyproject.toml` already lists pyMzLib under `readers` ("pyMzLib owns the parsing of producer
file formats"), so the call would come through a dependency you already accept. If running it at
all crosses your line, the alternative is **we run it and deliver the table**. Then `logs` is the
producer and you ingest as usual. Either way works for us.

## 3 · What the table is

It is long-format, with **one row per (accession, gene)** and never a pick. Every accession gets
exactly one of six outcomes:

- `resolved`
- `multi_gene`
- `off_primary_only` (only genes outside the primary assembly, i.e. your 009 §1 ALT trap, counted
  and not hidden)
- `not_in_source`
- `unrecognized_accession`
- `contaminant_not_mapped`

Each row carries:

- the verbatim `accession` and its `entry_accession`;
- `gene_stable_id` and `versioned_gene_id`;
- the release's `gene_symbol` and `gene_biotype`;
- `off_primary_genes`, the count of genes dropped as outside the set;
- `uniprot_gene_name`, as a label only;
- `ensembl_xref_agrees`, which separates UniProt's 350 multi-gene entries from Ensembl's 69 (our 010
  §2);
- provenance: `search_database_sha256`, `gene_set_release` and `gene_set_sha256`.

On your search database (sha `760984e8…`, 20,416 entries), it gives:

- **18,988** `resolved`
- **350** `multi_gene`
- **62** `off_primary_only`
- **1,016** `not_in_source`, which is exactly your 009 §1 zero-ENSG set

**REQ-DATAREPO-9.** Is that the shape you would store, keyed on
`(search_database_sha256, gene_set_release, accession, gene_stable_id)`? Is there any column you

## 4 · The two inputs

**The search database.** The resolution reads the XML itself, not just its sha. Your 009 §4 says
the file exists only on one machine (`F:/aging_data/db/…`) and that archiving it is aging's job.

**REQ-DATAREPO-10.** Can your pipeline reach that file when it resolves? Has aging answered your 041
about archiving it? If they haven't, option (b) in §2 is safer, because we have a verified copy.

**The gene set.** We will not ask you to carry a GTF. The human GTF is **141 MB** compressed and
**4.66 GB** unzipped. The resolver needs only its 78,941 gene rows and five fields each. A derived
gene-set file comes to **563 KB** for human (measured). We would publish one per
`(species, release)`, with the source GTF's name, sha256, release and genome build in its header, so
every row still traces back to an Ensembl file.

**Stating it plainly: that file and its loader are not built yet.** Today the resolver takes the
full GTF.
