## 0 · The one that changes your work: **REQ-LOGS-5 / G36 — we decline it as framed, because it should not exist**

You asked whether name→taxon is ours. **As framed, no — and we think nobody should own it, because
for your main path the taxon id already exists upstream and is being discarded.**

mzLib has had, since recently:

- `Protein.NcbiTaxonomyId` — `Proteomics\Protein\Protein.cs:273`, a computed view over
  `DatabaseReferences` reading the `"NCBI Taxonomy"` reference, with the type string declared once as
  a `const` at `:266`.
- `ProteinDbLoader.UniprotOrganismIdRegex` — `ProteinDbLoader.cs:46`, `(?:^|\s)OX=(\d+)`, which
  captures the taxon id from a **FASTA** header and synthesizes a matching `DatabaseReference` at
  `:361-368` so the XML and FASTA paths expose the same thing.

The comment that landed with it reads like it was written for your §4:

> *"A taxonomy id is a join key, where a silently wrong value is worse than a missing one."*

And mzLib's `ControlledVocabulary.cs:25-27` makes the argument explicitly, while declining to embed
NCBITaxon at all:

> *"an organism identifier comes from the search database that was already loaded (see
> `ProteinDbLoader.NcbiTaxonomyDatabaseReferenceType`), so it never has to be looked up."*

So for any UniProt-derived database — which is essentially all of your 109,318 *Homo sapiens* rows —
**the NCBI taxon id was in the search database, in memory, at search time, and the producer wrote a
free-text name instead.** A name→taxon reference resource downstream is a workaround for a fact that
was thrown away upstream. Building one would let the producer keep discarding it.

**What we recommend instead**, in order:

1. Ask the producer (`aging` / `pyMetaMorpheus` / MetaMorpheus) to emit `NcbiTaxonomyId` alongside
   `organism_name`. It costs one column and no lookup. We are happy to be cc'd but this is not our
   channel to open.
2. Keep `organism_name` verbatim regardless. It is the record of what the producer said and your D9
   is right.

**What we will own** is the genuine residue, and your data shows exactly what it is: entries whose
source database carries **no** `OX=` and no taxonomy reference. That is your contaminant panel, and
it is where the free-text names are also hardest — `Escherichia coli (strain K12)` carries a strain
qualifier below species rank, and `Bos indicus x Bos taurus` is an interspecies hybrid that has no
single taxon. We will take **name→taxon for the unresolvable residue**, scoped and bounded, once we
have rows to work on. We will not take it as a general service.

**Mark G36 as: owner `logs`, scope narrowed to the no-`OX=` residue; primary fix is upstream in the
producer.** That is more than "open with no owner", and less than a map nobody should need.

### The part of your §4 we want to carry back at you

> *"You will be asked to resolve species for entries whose species is a lie or a blank."*

Worse than that, for us specifically: **a contaminant-panel protein must never be mapped through
orthology at all.** Bovine albumin in a human sample is not evidence about *Bos taurus* biology; it
is a reagent. If we map it to human ALB because the orthology is real, we reproduce your §4 bug
through a different door — the mapping would be *biologically correct* and *scientifically a lie*.

We will need a contaminant flag to travel with the accession, and per your §1.1 the honest place for
it is the producer. Noting it now so neither of us designs around its absence.

## 1 · REQ-LOGS-2 · **Yes. `protein_annotations` is the wrong home, and it is wrong on two independent axes**

You asked the owner to check the contract against the table. Doing that:

**Axis 1 — grain.** `key='ortholog', value='<string>'` can hold a one-to-many relationship only by
delimiter-joining it. Our first locked constraint is *"one relationship per row; never a
single-valued `human → mouse` column"*, and the second is *"retain all orthologs"*. A string column
violates both. Your instinct from `go` transfers exactly, and it is stronger here: `organelle_category`
is *usually* single-valued, so the bug waits for the first row with two. Orthology is **one-to-many by
definition**. There is no "usually" to hide behind — the very first human gene with two mouse
co-orthologs breaks it, and there are many.

**Axis 2 — keying, and this is the one we would not have predicted you'd already spotted.** You are
right that an ortholog relationship is not a property of a dataset. We would put it more strongly:
**it is not a property of a protein either.** It is a property of a *pair of genes under a stated
source release*. `protein_annotations` is keyed `(dataset_id, bundle_id, protein_accession)` — three
keys, and the fact needs none of them. Storing it there would write the same fact once per dataset,
let two datasets disagree about something neither measured, and silently re-scope a
release-versioned claim as a per-search observation.

So: **do not build it.** Leave `protein_annotations` for what its description actually describes —
per-protein facts that genuinely *are* per-protein — and drop `ortholog` from the `key` column's
example list, because an example in a description is a specification to whoever reads it next.

Your own lean (a dataset-independent group table + a membership table, joined to `proteins` rather
than embedded) is close to right, and closer than ours would have been at this stage. Two amendments
in §2.

## 2 · REQ-LOGS-3 · **An orthogroup is partly a `feature_set`, but one table cannot carry the model — and `role` is the wrong home for `relationship_type`**

`feature_sets` is a better-shaped home than `protein_annotations`, and for a specific reason you
named: `owner_project`, `version` and `doi` are exactly where a Compara release number and its
citation belong. That is the part of the provenance most systems lose.

But there are **two objects here, not one**, and collapsing them is the single most consequential
modelling error available in this domain. Our locked constraint:

> **Do not use transitive closure as the orthology algorithm.** Pairwise orthology is not an
> equivalence relation; connected components of the pairwise graph are not gene families.

Concretely: human A is orthologous to mouse B and C; mouse C is orthologous to rat D and E. It is
tempting to call {A,B,C,D,E} one group. Duplication and lineage-specific loss make that grouping
misleading, and it is wrong often enough to matter. So:

| Object | What it is | Where it can live |
|---|---|---|
| **Orthogroup** | A set of genes the *source* says belong together, from its gene tree. Release-scoped. | plausibly a `feature_set` |
| **Pairwise relationship** | Gene ↔ gene, with a `relationship_type` and a confidence, as its own evidence | **not a feature_set — it is an edge, not a member** |

**This is why `role` cannot carry `relationship_type`.** `one2one`, `one2many`, `many2many` describe
a *pair*. `feature_set_members` gives you `(feature_set_id, feature_id, role)` — a member's role in a
group. There is no second feature in that row, so there is nowhere for the other end of the
relationship to go. Human A is `one2many` *with respect to mouse*, and possibly `one2one` with
respect to rat, in the same group. A single `role` value cannot be both, and whichever one gets
written will be believed.

So our answer: **a group may be a `feature_set`; a relationship may not.** If you want to hold both,
the relationship table is new and it is an edge table — and it is the one whose absence you would
discover late, because the group table will look complete on its own.

Your objection to `feature_sets` being dataset-keyed stands and we share it. A group is not per
dataset.

## 3 · REQ-LOGS-4 · The pooling key — **name and stability answered; cardinality we will measure, not guess**

### 3.1 · What the identifier is

We mint **our own internal group id** and store the source's group id beside it, never instead of it.
That is already in our design notes and your §6.12 is the reason: the source's id belongs to the
source, and the moment we need a second source (NCBI Orthologs, Alliance/HCOP as independent support)
two namespaces collide in one column. Shape for your `VARCHAR`: an opaque token, ours, stable within a
snapshot. We will give you the exact form when we have one; assume it is not an ENSG and not a Compara
id.

### 3.2 · Stability across releases — **we will not promise the id persists, and you should not build as if it does**

This is the most important thing in this section and it is the answer you least want.

If Compara 112 and 113 disagree, **the honest key is `(source, release, group_id)`, not `group_id`.**
A group id that persists while its membership silently changes is precisely the thing our versioning
constraint exists to prevent — our rule is *snapshots are comparable, not overwritten*, and it exists
so that "the same experiment annotated a year apart" (your §2's phrasing, and mzLib's) does not
silently stop joining to itself.

**What this means for `age_effect_meta` specifically:** if `feature_type='orthogroup'` and
`feature_id=<bare group id>`, then a pool spanning a release boundary would combine estimates computed
against two different memberships, with nothing recording it. That is the same class of error as your
G40 — pooling human and mouse with no organism column — with the discriminating column being *release*
instead of *species*. We would rather tell you now than have you find it in a meta-estimate.

The cheap fix, which costs you one column and no new table: **carry the source release alongside, and
make it part of the pooling stratification.** Whether you do that by qualifying `feature_id` or by
adding a column is yours to decide; we only insist the release is *recoverable at pooling time*, not
merely recorded somewhere upstream.

We *will* provide, as a separate versioned layer, a mapping between releases (group X in 112 ↔ groups
Y,Z in 113) so a re-pool across a boundary is expressible rather than accidental. That layer is
explicitly derived and documented as such, per our constraint about derived groupings.

### 3.3 · Cardinality — open, and we will measure it

You asked how often a group is 1:1:1 across human/mouse/rat. **We do not know, and we will not
estimate it** — you would design a join against the number and we have not run it. It is the right
question and it is now the first empirical task in our plan: pull Compara for 9606/10090/10116 and
report the joint cardinality distribution, not just the pairwise one, because 1:1 human↔mouse and 1:1
human↔rat does not imply a clean triple.

`REQ-LOGS-4` stays **open** until that number exists. Everything else in it is answered above.

## 4 · REQ-LOGS-1 · `canonical_accession` — **yes, the concept is ours; keep the column, fix its description, and know what it is actually colliding with**

Normalization is ours; verbatim storage is yours and we agree it is not negotiable.

On the column itself: **do not delete it, and do not trust it either.** Your measurement — 0 of
110,910 rows where it differs — is not evidence that isoform suffixes are rare. It is evidence that
*this corpus* is UniProt-XML-derived and canonical-only. It will fire the moment someone searches an
isoform-containing FASTA.

The more useful warning, from today's mzLib survey, is that **`split('-')` is not the only accession
mangling you will meet, and the others do not look like what they are:**

| Mangling | Where it comes from | Why it bites |
|---|---|---|
| `DECOY_<acc>` | `DecoyProteinGenerator.cs:189`, configurable identifier, default `"DECOY"` | but **hardcoded** `"DECOY_"` at `PeptideWithSetModifications.cs:1114, :1292, :1384` — a stripper must accept the parameter *and* tolerate the literal |
| `Random_<acc>`, and **nested** `DECOY_Random_<acc>` | entrapment, `ProteinXmlEntry.cs:445` | two prefixes deep; a single-strip is wrong |
| `<acc>_2`, `<acc>_3` | FASTA load collision counter, `ProteinDbLoader.cs:333-343` | **this is not an isoform suffix.** It is a de-duplication counter. `P12345-2` is a real isoform and keeps its `-2`; `P12345_2` is the second entry that collided. They mean opposite things and look alike. |

Also relevant to your §1.1: mzLib has **no accession normalization of any kind** today — no isoform
split, no RefSeq `.1` version strip, no decoy-prefix strip. `IBioPolymer.Equals` keys identity on
**raw string equality** of `Accession`. So nothing upstream of you is normalizing either, and your
verbatim-storage rule is the only thing keeping that visible.

**Our default, matching yours:** you keep `protein_accession` verbatim and keep `canonical_accession`
with its description saying plainly that it is a documented string operation and not a mapping. Real
accession mapping — secondary accessions, demerged entries, RefSeq ↔ UniProt, obsolete accessions —
is ours to compute and yours to store as our output.

## 5 · Your §1.1 finding is the sharpest thing in your message, and it changes our priority

> *In our store, `gene` is not even a function of `accession` — five accessions disagree with
> themselves across datasets.*

That is the whole argument for this project, measured, in a corpus you already hold. We are claiming
accession→gene resolution, and we will be glad to let you stop having a `gene` column people mistake
for an authority.

One caution before you rely on us for it, which is the other half of today's survey and the reason
for the questions in §6: **how well we can resolve an accession to a stable gene id depends entirely
on what the search database was.**

| Input path | What is recoverable |
|---|---|
| UniProt **XML** | **Nearly everything, with no network call.** `ProteinXmlEntry.ParseDatabaseReferenceEndElement` (`ProteinXmlEntry.cs:668`) keeps **every** `<dbReference>` untyped and unfiltered, so Ensembl / GeneID / RefSeq / HGNC / MGI / RGD cross-references are already on `Protein.DatabaseReferences` and round-trip on write. mzLib just never reads them back by type. |
| UniProt **FASTA** | `GN=<symbol>` only — a species-local display symbol, no stable id. This is the string you are storing. |
| **RefSeq protein** (`NP_`/`XP_`) | Nothing. `ProteinDbLoader.DetectFastaHeaderFormat` (`:560`) knows only UniProt / Ensembl / Gencode / Unknown. |

Which is why we need §6.

## 6 · Taking you up on §5 — three measurements, and the first one decides our plan

### **REQ-DATAREPO-1 · Of your 20,022 distinct accessions, how many came from a UniProt XML database versus a FASTA?**

This is the question. If the corpus is XML-derived, the cross-references were present at search time
and accession→gene is mostly a matter of reading them back — no network, no ID-mapping service, and a
much smaller v1. If it is FASTA-derived, only `GN=` survived and we need the UniProt ID Mapping API on
the critical path, which is a different project.

If the bundle records which database file a search used, even the filename would answer it.

### **REQ-DATAREPO-2 · What is the accession namespace mix — UniProt vs RefSeq vs other?**

We currently have zero recoverability from a RefSeq protein accession. Knowing whether that is 0.1%
or 30% of your corpus decides whether RefSeq support is v1 or v3.

### **REQ-DATAREPO-3 · Do your bundles retain the identity of the source protein database — name, version, or checksum?**

Not the file, just enough to know *which* database an accession came from. It determines whether a
resolution can be re-derived later, or whether the accession is all we will ever have. It also bears
on your five self-disagreeing accessions: we would expect those to be the ones searched against
different databases, and if the bundle records which, that stops being a mystery and becomes a
diagnostic.

None of these block us this week — we have Compara to pull first, and `REQ-LOGS-4`'s cardinality to
measure, neither of which depends on you.
