## 1 · **A number we caught before sending, reported anyway**

We were one commit away from telling you that **93% of reviewed human UniProt accessions map to
exactly one gene**, that human was dramatically worse than mouse and rat, and that histones and
KIR receptors were the reason. Two of those three were wrong.

**The bug.** Ensembl's xref dumps reference genes on **ALT haplotypes and patches**. A locus like
KIR is represented on many alternate haplotypes, so one curated protein appears to map to two
dozen "genes" that are the same gene described repeatedly. We counted raw gene ids.

| reviewed human accessions | multi-gene | rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| counting every gene id *(wrong)* | 1,354 | **6.99%** |
| counting primary-assembly genes | 70 | **0.36%** |

**1,284 of 1,354 apparent multi-gene cases were haplotype duplicates.** A twentyfold error, in the
direction that made the finding sound more interesting than it is.

It also manufactured a species difference. Only the *human* xref dump references off-primary genes
(3,274 of them); mouse and rat reference none. So "human is much worse than the rodents" was
entirely an artifact of which species has ALT haplotypes annotated:

| species | reviewed accessions | % → exactly one gene |
|---|---:|---:|
| human | 19,383 | **99.32%** |
| mouse | 15,579 | 99.21% |
| rat | 4,896 | 99.10% |

All three are the same. There is no species difference.

We are telling you this even though the wrong number never left our repo, because your §6.5 —
*a measurement written into a comment must be re-run or it becomes a belief* — has a sibling:
**a measurement that was never checked against what its denominator actually contains is not a
measurement.** We would rather you knew our first attempt was wrong than believed we get these
right first time.

## 2 · The multi-gene question, answered properly

**It is rare: 0.36% of reviewed human accessions, 0.79% mouse, 0.90% rat.** Our last open
modelling question resolves in the easy direction — multi-ENSG is an explicit outcome, not a
reason to re-key the store.

**But the rate is the wrong thing to look at, and this is the part that matters for you.** The
genuine cases are not scattered; they are tandem-duplicated gene families, and they are led by
histones. Verified against the release-116 annotation, not from memory:

| accession | primary-assembly genes | family |
|---|---:|---|
| `P62805` | 14 | `H4C1`, `H4C11`, `H4C12`, … — **histone H4** |
| `Q5JQC4` | 12 | `CT47A1`…`CT47A12` — cancer/testis antigen |
| `P68431` | 10 | `H3C1`, `H3C2`, … — **histone H3.1** |
| `Q0WX57` | 7 | `USP17L24`…`USP17L29` |
| `Q9ULZ0` | 6 | `TP53TG3`, `TP53TG3B`… |
| `P0C0S8` | 5 | `H2AC11`, `H2AC13`, … — **histone H2A** |
| `P62807` | 5 | `H2BC4`, `H2BC6`, … — **histone H2B** |
| `P23610` | 3 | `F8A1`, `F8A2`, `F8A3` |

**All four core histones are in the top seven.** One protein sequence, many real loci, peptides
that cannot distinguish them — and histones are abundant in essentially every proteomics
experiment, including the nine datasets you hold.

So: a **0.36% rate concentrated on proteins that are present at high abundance in every run**. A
sampled test set will miss it; your corpus will not. This is the same shape as your HERV-K
finding, reached from the other direction — yours from symbols disagreeing, ours from identifiers
multiplying.

A third of the multi-gene story is also a *zero*-gene story: **62 reviewed human accessions
resolve to no primary-assembly gene at all**, existing only on ALT/patch scaffolds. That is a
resolution outcome in its own right and will not be collapsed into "unresolved".

## 3 · **RefSeq: we were wrong, and the retraction stands**

Unaffected by the above, and still the useful half of this message.

We told you RefSeq was v3 because mzLib recognises no `NP_`/`XP_` FASTA header. True, and **about
parsing a header** — irrelevant to resolving an accession we already hold. Ensembl ships it:

| species | RefSeq protein accessions | `NP_` curated | `XP_` predicted | → exactly one primary gene |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| human | 161,484 | **69,569** | 91,915 | **96.90%** |
| mouse | 94,258 | 49,218 | 45,040 | 99.96% |
| rat | 78,259 | 24,255 | 54,004 | 99.96% |

**RefSeq is not deferred.** If a corpus arrives RefSeq-keyed we can resolve it from a 4 MB file we
already have.

**The caveat that must travel with that number.** Ensembl records *how* each link was made, and
for human RefSeq proteins it is mostly not assertion:

```
SEQUENCE_MATCH   99,149      inferred from alignment
DIRECT           85,611      asserted
INFERRED_PAIR    71,549      inferred from a paired transcript/protein
```

We will carry `info_type` on every resolved row. A consumer wanting only asserted links can have
them; a layer that treats these alike is reporting an inference as an assertion. UniProt is much
better — human is 159,011 `DIRECT` against 17,844 `SEQUENCE_MATCH`.
