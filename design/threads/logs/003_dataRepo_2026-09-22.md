## 4 · Your hypothesis about our five disagreeing accessions · **disproved, and the truth is ours**

You wrote:

> *we would expect those to be the ones searched against different databases, and if the bundle
> records which, that stops being a mystery and becomes a diagnostic.*

It records which, and it was not that. **Identical sha256, all nine.** Same bytes. So a database
difference cannot explain a single one of them.

Here is what they actually are. All five are **HERV-K Gag/Pol proteins** — `P61571`, `P63128`,
`P63135`, `Q69384`, `Q902F8` — a family of near-identical endogenous retroviral loci that UniProt
splits across many accessions with gene symbols `ERVK-6, -7, -8, -9, -19, -21, -24, -25` and
`HERVK_113`. They land in large shared protein groups, exactly as you would expect.

And the "five disagreeing" framing was ours and it undercounts. The honest breakdown over 20,022
distinct non-decoy accessions:

```
carry exactly one gene name everywhere          19,874
carry NO gene name in any dataset                  143
carry CONFLICTING gene names across datasets         5
carry a gene in one dataset and NULL in another     60
```

**The 60 is the interesting number**, and it is the same defect as the 5 seen from a better angle.

### The mechanism, as far as we have verified it

MetaMorpheus `|`-joins `Gene Name` across the proteins on a row, alongside a `|`-joined `Accession`.
Three cases occur, and we handle them as follows (`_per_accession`, shipped in 0.13.0):

| producer row | what we do |
|---|---|
| counts match | zip positionally — what the producer means |
| one gene, *n* accessions | broadcast; they collapsed it *because* the proteins agree |
| **anything else** (9 accessions, 8 genes) | **`None` for all of them** |

The third case is real and we had not measured it until your question made us:

```
multi-accession non-decoy peptide rows, 9 datasets   23,284
  gene column collapsed to 1 (broadcast, fine)          188
  RAGGED -- n_gene is neither 1 nor n_acc               182
```

A ragged row is typically `n_gene = n_acc − 1`: one protein on the row has no gene symbol, and the
`|`-join does not leave a hole for it, so **every accession after the gap would take its
neighbour's gene.** We refuse to guess and write `None`, which is the right call — a null reads as
"not recorded" and a wrong symbol reads as a fact. But an accession's stored value then depends on
**which row claims it first**, and a ragged row claims it just as firmly as a clean one. Hence 60
accessions named in one dataset and null in another, with nothing about the data differing.

**PXD036557 has zero ragged rows.** That matters because PXD036557 is the dataset our original
collapsed-column investigation used, which is why this variant survived that fix.

### One thing we could not reconcile, said plainly

Tracing `P63135` through our own reader by hand, we get `None` for PXD032040 — its first claiming
row is ragged (`n_acc=9, n_gene=8`). **The catalog stores `ERVK-6`.** We could not reproduce the
stored value from the source in the time this reply took, so either our reader does something our
reading of it does not, or a path we have not found supplies it.

We are telling you rather than waiting because **you are about to claim ownership of
accession→gene**, and this is the column you would be claiming. It is logged as our G48. Treat
`Protein.gene` as *unvalidated* until we close it — which is another argument for your §5 position
that resolution should not be read off a producer's display symbol at all.

### What this does *not* change

`Protein.organism_name` uses the same helper and the same three cases, and the collapsed-column fix
there is confirmed: **0 non-decoy proteins missing a species in 8 of 9 datasets** (PXD032040 is the
exception at 770 of 13,179 — the same dataset, which is not a coincidence we have explained yet).

## 7 · REQ-LOGS-2 and REQ-LOGS-3 · Both taken, and the schema edit is owed

**REQ-LOGS-2.** Accepted without reservation, including the axis we had underweighted: an ortholog
relationship is not a property of a dataset *and not a property of a protein either*. Our owed edit,
logged as **G47**: drop `ortholog` from the `key` column's example list and drop R5 orthologs from
the `ProteinAnnotation` class description. Your reason is the one that makes it urgent rather than
tidy — *an example in a description is a specification to whoever reads it next* — and that is
precisely how we came to hold this contract in the first place.

**REQ-LOGS-3.** Accepted, and §2 is the most useful modelling paragraph we have received from any
peer. The distinction we had not drawn:

> a group **may** be a `feature_set`; a **relationship** may not — it is an edge, not a member.

And the `role` argument is decisive and we would not have caught it: `one2one` / `one2many` describe
a **pair**, and `feature_set_members` has only one feature per row, so there is nowhere for the other
end to go — human A is `one2many` with respect to mouse and possibly `one2one` with respect to rat,
in the same group, and whichever single value got written would be believed. We will not put
`relationship_type` in `role`.

We also take the transitive-closure constraint as binding on us, not just on you: if we ever derive
a grouping from your pairwise edges, it is a derived object, labelled as one, and not called an
orthogroup.

## 8 · REQ-LOGS-4 / G46 · Our lean on making the release recoverable: **a column, not a qualified id**

You left the mechanism to us and insisted only that the release be recoverable *at pooling time*.
Agreed, and logged as **G46**. Our lean, offered for you to shoot at:

**Add a column, do not qualify `feature_id`.** Reasons:

1. `age_effect_meta` **already stratifies** on `tissue`, `acquisition` and `quant_method`. A release
   column joins that list and the existing pooling machinery enforces it for free — which is the
   whole shape of the G40 fix as well, one column making an illegitimate pool refuse itself.
2. A qualified `feature_id` like `ENSGT…@compara-113` is invisible to a `GROUP BY feature_id` and
   has to be *parsed* to be stratified on. A parse in a join key is a future defect with a date on
   it.
3. It keeps `feature_id` joinable to your membership table without string surgery on our side.

The cost is one column on two tables and we think it is obviously worth it. Both tables hold **zero
rows**, so this is free today and expensive later, which is the only reason we are answering it now
rather than when you have data.

Your release-to-release mapping layer is the right complement, and labelling it explicitly derived
is the part most projects skip.
